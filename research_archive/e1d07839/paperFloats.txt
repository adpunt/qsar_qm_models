## 1. RESEARCH QUESTIONS (verbatim)

### 1a. Abstract (L164, L167)
- L164: "While the impact of noisy labels on QSAR modeling has been studied, the relative contributions of the choice of molecular representation and machine learning model architecture remains unclear."
- L164: "While noise is typically modeled as Gaussian, we developed six noise injection strategies to investigate other patterns of noise."
- L167 (Scientific contribution): "We demonstrate that the choice of model, rather than molecular representation, is the primary determinant of QSAR-model noise robustness, a conclusion we reached by comparing a model's relative ranking across different types of label noise."

### 1b. Introduction — the three questions, all in L188
- L188: "This research addresses three main aspects of the robustness of QSAR models under label noise."
- Q1, L188: "First, we investigate the contributions of molecular representation and model architecture on both overall predictive performance and, more specifically, noise robustness."
- Q2, L188: "Second, we compare how noise-robust probabilistic models are versus their deterministic counterparts, and whether their per-sample uncertainty estimates track which individual labels have been corrupted, once the population-level rise in average uncertainty at each noise level is controlled for."
- Q3, L188: "Third, we assess the generalizability of noise-robustness patterns across different noise-injection mechanisms and molecular properties."
- L188: "Together, these questions aim to identify what, if anything, makes a QSAR model robust to noise."
- L188: "Finally, we introduce NoiseInject, an open-source Python package for benchmarking noise robustness and uncertainty quantification in machine learning models."

### 1c. Conclusion (§Conclusion, L569–577) — restated as findings, not questions
- L571: "We investigated how a wide variety of molecular representations and model architectures perform under a regime of increasing label noise in QSAR modeling."
- L577: "When working with noisy experimental data in QSAR settings, it is important to keep in mind that noise robustness and noise-aware uncertainty estimation are primarily driven by the model's mechanism of training, and to a certain extent the choice of molecular representation, usually with respect to the model."

### 1d. Also stated in Results (interim "conclusions", L562, L564)
- L562: "These results lead to two main conclusions. The first is that noise robustness, defined as the rate at which predictive performance degrades with increasing label noise, is primarily determined by the model's training mechanism."
- L564: "The second conclusion concerns whether a model's uncertainty estimates track per-sample label noise."
