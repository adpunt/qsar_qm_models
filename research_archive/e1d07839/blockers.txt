# (A) DECISIONS THAT CHANGE WHAT GETS COMPUTED

## A1. Should the validation labels carry noise too? — HIGHEST COST

**Question.** After the uncommitted Rust fix, only the training split gets noise; but six model families paste the validation set straight into their training set, so roughly one in nine of their training labels is clean — while the neural nets instead early-stop against a clean validation signal. Do we noise the validation split, stop merging it, or leave it?

**Why irreversible.** It changes the fitted model for RF, QRF, SVM, NGBoost, XGBoost, LightGBM, GP and conformal-GP, and the stopping epoch for every neural net. Every R², every `auc_norm`, every uncertainty CSV, on QM9 *and* on the three external datasets. There is no post-hoc correction.

**Evidence.**
- `rust/src/main.rs:1029` train `true`, `:1056` val `false`, `:1083` test `false`; guard at `:750`; noise indices are `0..train_count` only, `rust/src/main.rs:1142-1147`.
- Unconditional merges: `models/models.py:1382-1383` (RF/QRF), `:1457-1458` (SVM), `:1501-1507` + `:1518` (NGBoost), `:1622-1623` (XGBoost), `:1672-1673` (LightGBM), `:1732-1738` (GP), `:3576-3577` (conformal-GP, which then computes conformity scores on `x_val` at `:3671` — inside its own fit set).
- Split is 80/10/10 (`scripts/process_and_train.py:495-500`), so the merged training set is 11.1% clean labels for those families and 0% for the NNs.
- NN side: `train_nn` early-stops on `val_loss` (`models/models.py:1871-1879`) and **never restores best weights** — it returns the model at the break epoch.
- External pipeline has the same asymmetry from the other direction: `KIRBy/tests/alternative_data_noise_robustness.py:980-981` takes `y_val` from `y_train_full` *before* injection, and `:760`/`:795` inject into `y_train` only — so neural nets there early-stop on a clean oracle, and trees never see val at all (`run_tree_experiment`, `:750`, takes no val).

**Options.**
1. **Noise train + val, test stays clean.** Every family trains on 100% corrupted labels; early stopping and calibration happen against corrupted labels, as in real practice. Changes: `rust/src/main.rs:1142-1147` (draw a second map over val indices), `:1056` (`apply_noise` true), and the same in `KIRBy/tests/alternative_data_noise_robustness.py:978-981` (inject before carving val).
2. **Delete the merges, keep val clean.** Trees lose 11% of their data; NNs and trees both then hold a clean oracle, which is a leak the paper cannot defend. Changes: the eight merge sites listed above.
3. **Leave as is.** The paper's headline ("tree ensembles are the most robust") is then partly manufactured by giving exactly those models clean labels the neural nets never get.

**Recommendation: option 1.** It is the only configuration where "robustness" measures what the paper says it measures, and it makes the two pipelines comparable.

## A2. Does the threshold cut move to the standardised label?

**Question.** Threshold noise is supposed to hit molecules with extreme labels, but the code compares the raw label to ±1.0 — on QM9 that is every molecule, so threshold is just Gaussian noise at double strength. Do we move the cut onto the standardised label?

**Why irreversible.** All threshold runs — 144 of the 864 `anova_*.csv` targets on QM9, plus the threshold sixth of all three external datasets. Existing threshold results are not comparable to fixed ones.

**Evidence.**
- Raw comparison: `rust/src/main.rs:364-373` reads `target_values[idx]`, which is the raw f32 from `read_all_target_values` (`:542`); normalisation happens later and separately at `:759-761`. Defaults ±1.0 at `:1195-1196`.
- Same raw comparison in the external injector: `NoiseInject/noiseInject/core.py:97-115`.
- Consequence on the actual training set (`qm9[:10000]`, `scripts/process_and_train.py:625`): min gap 2.084 eV → **100%** of molecules get 2σ, threshold CV of σ_i = 0.000 exactly. Per-dataset: LogD 85.4%, hERG 100%, log10 Caco-2 efflux 9.2% — so the same strategy is a pure dose increase on three datasets and a concentrated tail-attack on the fourth.
- `paper.tex:354` states the cut is "$|y| > 1.0$ (on normalized data)". That is factually false against the code.

**Options.**
1. **Standardise before the cut** (`z_i = (y-ȳ)/s`, cut at |z|>1). Threshold becomes a genuinely distinct mechanism, comparable across endpoints; matches the caption. Changes: `rust/src/main.rs:364-373` (+ pass `mean`/`std_dev`, already computed at `:973-978`), `NoiseInject/noiseInject/core.py:97-115`.
2. **Keep raw, rewrite the paper.** Zero compute; but you must state that threshold ≡ Gaussian at 2σ on QM9 and hERG, and every threshold-vs-Gaussian comparison in Results becomes a dose comparison.
3. **Drop threshold** from the six.

**Recommendation: option 1**, done in both injectors in the same commit. Reason: it is the difference between six mechanisms and four mechanisms plus two relabelled doses.

## A3. Should σ be renormalised so one unit means the same amount of corruption in every strategy?

**Question.** At σ=1 the six strategies deliver RMS label perturbations from 0.502 to 2.000 eV — a 4× range — so every cross-strategy comparison in the paper is currently a comparison of doses, not of noise patterns. Do we rescale each strategy so σ means the same corruption everywhere?

**Why irreversible.** Rescaling changes the injected values, so every σ>0 point for five of the six strategies, on every dataset. It also changes the meaning of the σ axis, hence `auc_norm` (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1798-1800`, trapezoid over σ normalised by the σ range).

**Evidence.**
- Formulas and defaults: valprop `main.rs:309-317` / `:1176-1177` (factor 0.1); quantile `:322-352` / `:1184-1188`; threshold `:355-376` / `:1195-1199`; outlier `:379-404` / `:1206-1208`; hetero `:406-415` / `:1215-1216`.
- Realised RMS dose at σ=1 on the first-8000 QM9 training set: legacy 1.000, threshold 2.000, valprop 1.701, quantile 0.899, hetero 0.669, outlier 0.502.
- Dose ordering re-orders across endpoints (threshold is the harshest strategy on QM9 and LogD, second-mildest on Caco-2).
- Mean retention per strategy is a perfect rank-inversion of the dose ordering (Spearman −1.000 on QM9; −0.943 / −1.000 / −0.943 on LogD / Caco-2 / hERG). The paper's per-strategy η² differences are dose differences.
- `paper.tex:222` and `paper.tex:354` both promise the opposite ("the difficulty scaling controlled by σ is consistent").

**Options.**
1. **Renormalise each strategy to a common realised dose** (scale σ_i so that RMS(ε) = σ·SD(y), computed from the training labels before injection). Cross-strategy differences then isolate *shape*. Legacy is unchanged; the other five re-run. One extra pass over the label array in `main.rs:302-430` and `NoiseInject/noiseInject/core.py:71-170`.
2. **Keep raw σ, report realised dose alongside**, and delete every claim that compares strategies as mechanisms (paper L380, L383, L460, L462, L493, L562). Zero compute; the paper loses Q3.
3. **Post-hoc dose-matching in analysis only** (interpolate each strategy's curve to matched dose). Cheap, but the σ grids do not overlap in dose at either end — the σ=1 outlier point has no Gaussian counterpart below σ=0.502, so a third of every curve is extrapolated.

**Recommendation: option 1.** It is the only version in which Q3 ("do robustness patterns generalise across noise mechanisms?") is answerable, and it is a one-time change to two files. Decide this **before** A6, because it redefines the σ axis.

## A4. Do we save per-sample uncertainty for training molecules — and is RQ2 salvageable at all?

**Question.** The paper claims uncertainty estimates track which individual labels were corrupted; but corrupted labels only ever enter training, and uncertainty is only ever saved for test molecules — which the model never saw a label for. Do we add training-molecule uncertainty, and if so, cross-fitted or in-sample?

**Why irreversible.** No file on disk permits reconstructing it: the mmaps carrying both clean and noisy training targets are deleted at `scripts/process_and_train.py:1826-1835`, the noise map is never written (built `rust/src/main.rs:1242-1247`, dropped at exit), and the normalisation constants are returned in-process only (`:973-978`).

**Evidence.**
- All 13 `save_uncertainty_values` call sites pass test arrays: `models/models.py:1403, 1555, 1793, 2155, 2272, 2494, 2761, 3196, 3397, 4000, 6851, 6977, 7183`. `grep "predict(x_train"` in `models/models.py` returns nothing.
- Writer: `scripts/utils.py:200-269`; `injected_noise` is a linear-regression residual (`:216-224`), not a recorded ε.
- **The metric is undefined by construction on held-out data.** A model's uncertainty at a test molecule cannot depend on that molecule's label perturbation, because the perturbation never entered training. Pre-fix, `injected_noise` on test was the *training* molecule's draw at the same index (`rust/src/main.rs:740-748`, loop counter restarts at `:625`) — i.e. a correlation against a permutation. Post-fix it is identically zero. Neither is an answer.
- The external half cannot help: no `injected_noise` column exists there at all (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1218`, `:1252-1254`).

**Options.**
1. **Out-of-fold cross-fitted uncertainty on the training set.** K-fold inside train; each molecule scored by the fold that excluded it. The only statistically honest version. Costs K× the uncertainty arm (~544 invocations today carry `-u True`) and is a protocol change, not a re-run.
2. **In-sample training-set uncertainty**, degeneracy stated. Cheap (one duplicated predict block per model, `scripts/utils.py:200-210` + `scripts/process_and_train.py:1815-1819` + `models/models.py:1387-1418, 1533-1570, 1770-1810, 2073-2172, 2680-2778`). But GP posterior variance collapses at its own training inputs by construction, and RF/QRF are fit on train ∪ val — near-zero spread on their own rows. The answer will be "no signal" for reasons that have nothing to do with noise.
3. **Retire RQ2 as posed; report the population-level questions that the existing data already answers.** Uncertainty inflates ~2× while error inflates ~1.24× (27/27 configs); error-ranking collapses (ρ 0.289 → 0.069, 27/27); 1σ coverage drifts 0.635 → 0.884 (27/27); across-molecule CV of uncertainty falls 0.398 → 0.156 (27/27) — uncertainty inflates uniformly, not selectively. All from `/Users/apunt/repos/KIRBy/tests/results_server/validation_rerun/*/*/{QRF,NGBoost,GP}_*_uncertainty_values.csv`, no compute.

**Recommendation: option 3 now, option 1 only if a fourth uncertainty arm is affordable.** Reason: RQ2 as written is unanswerable in principle on held-out data, and the answerable version is already fully supported by 27 files sitting on disk.

**If you take option 1 or 2**, also dump exact ε: `rust/src/main.rs` between `:1247` and `:1252`, write `train_index,noise_raw` plus `mean,std_dev` (both in scope at `:1249-1250`). Otherwise `injected_noise` stays a regression estimate.

## A5. Do the Bayesian networks get a two-output (mean + variance) head?

**Question.** Nothing in the model roster predicts a per-molecule noise level — the component that would actually flag a corrupted label — so do the neural families get a mean-and-variance output head?

**Why irreversible.** Different architecture and different loss; every neural run.

**Evidence.**
- `scripts/utils.py:80` sampling BNNs return `aleatoric = None`; `:162` NGBoost/QRF return `epistemic = None`; `:130` GP aleatoric is `np.full_like(...)`, one constant; `:188` VBLL the same.
- The two-output head already exists — `models/models.py:2031` (`model.fc3 = nn.Linear(hidden_size2, 2)` under `loss_name == 'heteroscedastic'`) — but column 1 is thrown away at `:2088` and `:2103` (`output = output[:, 0:1]`), and `decompose_uncertainty_sampling_heteroscedastic` (`scripts/utils.py:88`) has **zero callers repo-wide**.
- `--loss` is never passed by any SLURM script (default `mse`, `scripts/process_and_train.py:291`), so the head has never been active.
- The MLP-side override (`models/models.py:2621-2632`) tests only for `fc_out`/`output_layer`; `FactorizationMLP` and `MTLRegressionModel` have neither, so it is a silent no-op there — a 1-output net trained against a 2-output loss.
- Two models already compute a genuine per-molecule aleatoric term and discard it: `train_heteroscedastic_gp` (`:6835-6837` computed, only `total_std` saved at `:6851`) and `train_evidential_kernel` (`:6963-6964` computed, `:6977` saves total only).

**Options.**
1. **Turn on the two-output head for the NN families** as an added arm. Doubles the neural matrix and requires fixing `:2088`/`:2103` and wiring `decompose_uncertainty_sampling_heteroscedastic`, plus the `FactorizationMLP`/`MTL` no-op.
2. **Keep one output; drop the per-sample aleatoric claim** and say plainly that no model in the roster predicts an input-dependent noise level.
3. **Two-line fix instead:** pass the already-computed components at `models/models.py:6851` and `:6977`, and add `het_gp_*` / `evidential_kernel_*` to the figure roster (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:283-289`).

**Recommendation: 2 + 3.** No architecture change; you get a real per-molecule aleatoric arm for two models at essentially zero cost, and the honest statement for the rest.

## A6. Does the σ grid change?

**Question.** Keep 11 levels (0.0 to 1.0 in steps of 0.1), or coarsen / re-centre on realistic assay error?

**Why irreversible.** `auc_norm` is a trapezoid over the grid divided by the grid range (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1798-1800`); configs need ≥3 σ points and a σ=0 row (`:1820-1832`). Numbers from two different grids cannot appear in one table.

**Evidence.** 1252 of 1259 slurm invocations use exactly the 11-point grid; the only exceptions are a 7-point resume (`slurm_scripts_fixup_timeout/anova_cpdv_svm_valprop.sh:24`) and two validation smoke tests. Published assay-error SDs put one unit of real experimental error near σ≈0.6, so roughly half the grid sits beyond anything physical.

**Options.**
1. **Keep 11 points.** Comparable to all existing tables; full cost.
2. **Coarsen to 6 points (0, 0.2, …, 1.0).** ~45% saving on the whole matrix. `auc_norm` shifts by trapezoid error; the entire results set must move together.
3. **Re-centre on 0–0.6 with finer spacing.** Best physical interpretation, but abandons the "extreme noise" half of every degradation curve.

**Recommendation: option 1** if A9's budget allows; otherwise **option 2** applied uniformly. Do not mix grids. Decide after A3, since renormalisation changes what a σ unit is.

## A7. Does the skewed-noise experiment run?

**Question.** Do we run the non-Gaussian noise *shapes* (left-tailed, right-tailed, U-shaped) at all?

**Why irreversible.** A separate arm, multiplying the matrix by however many distributions are added.

**Evidence.** `--distribution` is never passed by any of the 30 slurm directories; default `gaussian` (`scripts/process_and_train.py:241`). The implementations are wrong:
- LeftTailed (`rust/src/main.rs:465-471`): both branches return ≤ 0 — support is (−∞, 0], i.e. a systematic downward label shift, not zero-mean noise.
- RightTailed (`:480-486`): `powf(1.5)` on the positive side, `powf(0.5)` on the negative — realised SD ≠ σ and not linear in σ.
- UShaped (`:491-496`): Var = 6σ², so SD ≈ 2.449σ; the comment at `:260-262` claims σ².
- Only Uniform (`:498-502`) is correct. All of it is duplicated at `:211-264`.

**Options.**
1. **Do not run; delete the three broken variants and the duplicate block.** Zero cost, removes a landmine.
2. **Fix them (zero-mean, unit-scale) and run as a supplementary arm.** Three extra distributions across the matrix; and each is a fresh dose confound on top of A3.
3. Run as is — guaranteed-wrong results.

**Recommendation: option 1.** Nothing in the paper claims them.

## A9. What is the scope of the QM9 re-run?

**Why irreversible.** It is the budget.

**Evidence.**

**Options.**

## A10. Do the deterministic models keep running six times at σ=0?

**Question.** At σ=0 no noise is injected regardless of strategy, so six of the strategy runs collapse to one for the deterministic models — do we stop paying for the duplicates?

**Why irreversible.** It is ~9% of the compute bill (one of eleven σ levels, five-sixths wasted for part of the roster), and it inflates every count on pooled σ=0 data sixfold.

**Evidence.** `scripts/process_and_train.py:1609` sets `'noise': s > 0`, and `rust/src/main.rs:750` gates on `config.noise`, so σ=0 is byte-identical across strategies. Confirmed empirically: LightGBM, QRF, SVM and XGBoost have exactly zero across-strategy SD at σ=0 in **100%** of (model, rep, fold) cells on all three external datasets; RF 80/70/5%; every neural model 0% (they re-seed).

**Options.**
1. **Run σ=0 once per (model, rep) and reuse it** across strategies for the deterministic families. Saves ~9%; needs a change in the strategy loop of each slurm script.
2. **Keep the duplication, deduplicate in analysis.** No compute saving, but fixes the statistics.
3. Keep as is — every σ=0 standard error, df and p-value is inflated 6× for half the roster.

**Recommendation: option 2** if editing 216 slurm scripts is unattractive, **option 1** if the budget is tight. Either way the deduplication must reach the analysis.
