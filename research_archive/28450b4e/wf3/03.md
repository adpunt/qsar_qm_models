### 1. Wenlock MC, Potter T, Barton P, Austin RP (2011) — **PEER-REVIEWED. STRONGEST SOURCE.**

**Citation:** Wenlock MC, Potter T, Barton P, Austin RP. "A Method for Measuring the Lipophilicity of Compounds in Mixtures of 10." *Journal of Biomolecular Screening* 2011;16(3):348–355. DOI: 10.1177/1087057110396372. Affiliation: Department of Chemistry, AstraZeneca R&D Charnwood, Loughborough, UK.
**Access:** (R) Full text read — open PDF at journals.sagepub.com, converted with pdftotext.
**What was measured:** 1-octanol/water logD7.4, automated shake-flask + LC/MS/MS, compounds pooled in mixtures of up to 10.

Four separate error figures, all (R):

**(a) Day-to-day assay reproducibility, N = 287 compounds — the headline number**
> "During the application of the mixture-based method to lipophilicity screening, repeat data on 287 compounds that were run on 2 different days were collected. Figure 3 shows a plot of the log D7.4 values determined on the second date of testing against those determined on the first test date. **The RMSE between these two data sets is 0.21 log units**, and a two-tailed paired t-test indicates that there is no significant difference between the sets of data (p = 0.45, tstat = 0.76, tcritical at 5% significance level = 1.97)."

**(b) Within-assay precision, 200 repeat determinations of one compound (cyclobenzaprine)**
> "To test the assay-to-assay precision of the mixture-based shake flask technique, the log D7.4 of cyclobenzaprine was run in 200 separate assays, where on each occasion it was present in mixtures that contained different sets of compounds. The mean log D7.4 for these measurements is 2.74 with a **standard deviation of 0.13**."
> "The small standard deviation of 0.13 indicates that any interactions between cyclobenzaprine and the other components of the many mixtures it has been measured in cannot be leading to significant shifts in the observed data."
(R) Data normally distributed, Shapiro-Wilk p = 0.85.

**(c) Agreement with independent literature values, N = 22 compounds (this is a de facto inter-laboratory / inter-protocol comparison)**
> "The root mean square error (RMSE) between the log D7.4 values generated using our method for single compounds, excluding the out-of-range values, and the previously reported data is **0.21 log units**."
> "…the automated experimental procedure, when applied to single compounds, is able to reproduce literature data with a satisfactory level of random error (i.e., **RMSE <0.3 log units**) and with no evidence of significant systematic error."

**(d) Mixture vs. single-compound measurement, N = 22**
> "The RMSE between the mixture and single data is **0.18 log units**…"

**(C) Derived single-measurement SD:** an RMSE of 0.21 between two *independent single* determinations of the same quantity implies a single-measurement standard deviation of 0.21/√2 = **0.148 log units**. This arithmetic is mine, not stated in the paper.

### 2. OECD Test Guideline 107 (shake flask) — **REGULATORY GUIDELINE, VERIFIED VERBATIM**

**Citation:** OECD (1995). *OECD Guideline for the Testing of Chemicals, Test No. 107: Partition Coefficient (n-octanol/water): Shake Flask Method.* Adopted by the Council 27 July 1995. OECD Publishing, Paris. DOI: 10.1787/9789264069626-en.
**Access:** (R) Official OECD PDF read in full (9789264069626-en.pdf).

**The ±0.3 criterion you asked me to verify — CONFIRMED:**
> "18. A POW value is calculated from the data of each run. Altogether six values are obtained since the test conditions are: three duplicate runs with different solvent ratios, and possibly also different quantities of test substance. **The six log POW values should fall within a range of ± 0.3 units.**"

**Stated working range:**
> "2. POW values in the range log POW between −2 and 4 (occasionally up to 5) can be experimentally determined by the method set out in this guideline (1)(2). POW values in the range log POW between 0 and 6 can be estimated using high performance liquid chromatography…"

(I) ±0.3 is a *spread/acceptance* criterion on six determinations within one lab, not a standard deviation. Quoting it as "the experimental error of logP" would be a misuse.

### 3. OECD Test Guideline 117 (HPLC) — **REGULATORY GUIDELINE, VERIFIED VERBATIM. This is the inter-laboratory number.**

**Citation:** OECD (2022). *OECD Guidelines for the Testing of Chemicals, Test Guideline No. 117: Partition Coefficient (n-octanol/water), HPLC Method.* 30 June 2022. OECD Publishing, Paris. DOI: 10.1787/9789264069824-en.
**Access:** (R) Official OECD PDF read in full.

> "5. QUALITY CRITERIA
> 13. In order to increase the confidence in the measurement, duplicate determinations must be made.
> **Repeatability: The value of log Pow derived from repeated measurements made under identical conditions and using the same set of reference substances should fall within a range of + 0.1 log units.**
> **Reproducibility: If the measurements are repeated with a different set of reference substances, results may differ.** Typically, the correlation coefficient R for the relationship between log k and log Pow for a set of test substances is around 0.9, corresponding to an octanol/water partition coefficient of log Pow **+ 0.5 log units**."

> "14. **The inter-laboratory comparison test has shown that with the HPLC method log Pow values can be obtained to within + 0.5 units of the Shake-Flask values (2).** Other comparisons can be found in the literature (4)(5)(9)(10)(11)."

(I) The "+" in all three places is the ± glyph; pdftotext maps it to "+" throughout this file (no "±" extracts anywhere from TG 117, whereas TG 107 extracts "±" correctly). Read as ±0.1 and ±0.5.
(R) TG 117 working range: log Pow 0 to 6.

## THE SINGLE MOST DEFENSIBLE NUMBER

> **Typical experimental error of a logD7.4 measurement is ≈0.2 log units (RMSE between independent repeat determinations), corresponding to a single-measurement standard deviation of ≈0.15 log units.**

**Cite:** Wenlock MC, Potter T, Barton P, Austin RP. *J Biomol Screen* 2011;16(3):348–355. DOI: 10.1177/1087057110396372.

**Quote to use:** "The RMSE between these two data sets is 0.21 log units" (n = 287 compounds, same compounds measured on two different days, AstraZeneca automated 1-octanol/water shake-flask logD7.4).

**Why this one:** it is the only figure I verified that is (i) peer-reviewed, (ii) logD7.4 specifically rather than logP, (iii) drug-like compounds rather than environmental chemicals, (iv) a genuine repeat-measurement dispersion rather than an acceptance criterion, and (v) built on a real sample (287 compounds, plus an independent 200-replicate single-compound check giving SD = 0.13, which agrees).

**Caveats you must state alongside it (I):**
1. **This is a within-laboratory, single-optimised-assay number — a lower bound.** The cross-laboratory / cross-method figure is larger: OECD TG 117 states HPLC-derived log Pow agrees with shake-flask only "to within ±0.5 units" in inter-laboratory comparison. A public dataset aggregating multiple labs and methods will sit above 0.15 and plausibly near 0.3–0.5.
2. **Defensible bracket to quote:** ~0.15 log units (single well-controlled assay, Wenlock 2011) to ~0.5 log units (inter-laboratory / inter-method, OECD TG 117). Both endpoints are verbatim-sourced above.
3. **There is no peer-reviewed round-robin study of drug-like logD7.4 across laboratories that I could verify.** The only true multi-lab ring test I found (Tolls 2003) is on four superhydrophobic environmental chemicals. If you need "inter-laboratory reproducibility of logD7.4", the honest statement is that no such peer-reviewed study was located; the OECD TG 117 ±0.5 statement is the closest regulatory proxy.
4. **The "0.27–0.62 log units for logD" figure carried in your project memory is not supported by anything I verified in this search.** Nothing I found produces that range. It needs re-tracing to a source or replacing.

**Relevance to your noise injection (C, arithmetic only):** against a single-assay error of σ_exp ≈ 0.15 log units, an injected σ = 0.6 is ≈4× one unit of real assay error; against the inter-laboratory/inter-method figure of 0.5, σ = 0.6 is ≈1.2×. Your σ grid should be described relative to whichever of these two you adopt, and the choice stated explicitly — they differ by more than 3×.

**Highest-value follow-up if you can get ACS access:** Wenlock & Carlsson 2015, DOI 10.1021/ci500535s (AZ logD7.4 among eight DMPK endpoints, ~30 years of pooled assay data — almost certainly contains the definitive per-endpoint "typical stdev"), and Sheridan et al. 2020, DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.9b01067 (Merck LOGD experimental-error estimate).
