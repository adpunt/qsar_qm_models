### 1. hERG-SPECIFIC ASSAY VARIABILITY

#### 1a. Alvarez Baron et al. 2025 — multi-laboratory manual patch clamp (BEST hERG-SPECIFIC SOURCE)

- **Citation:** Alvarez Baron C, Zhao J, Yu H, et al. "Multi-laboratory comparisons of manual patch clamp hERG data generated using standardized protocols and following ICH S7B Q&A 2.1 best practices." *Scientific Reports* 2025;15:29995. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-15761-8. PMC12357877.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES (Scientific Reports, research article). Open access.
- **Measured:** hERG IC50 for **28 drugs** across **5 laboratories** (B'SYS, CiPA Lab, Charles River, Drug Safety Testing Center Japan, FDA), manual whole-cell patch clamp, standardized protocol, blinded, two phases of 14 drugs.
- **Error figures (R):**
  - Residual (meta-analysis τ): **SD = 0.18 pIC50 log units**
  - Total spread: **~5× IC50** (≈0.7 log units, 2.5th–97.5th percentile)
  - Within-experiment median SD by lab: **0.18–0.24 pIC50 units**
- **Verbatim quotes (R):**
  > "The estimated overall variability (τ) not explained by drug and laboratory and expressed as SD was 0.18, and as 95% CI 0.69, corresponding to an IC50 ratio of 4.9X (95% CI: 4.0 to 6.2)."

  > "The difference between the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles for these values was 0.7, the antilog of which is 5."

  > "hERG block potency values within 5X of each other should not be considered different, since these values are within the natural data distribution of the hERG assay; laboratory-specific safety margin threshold may be required to account for systematic data differences."

  Per-laboratory median within-experiment pIC50 SD (Fig. 7): Lab 1 **0.23** (2.5/97.5 pct: 0.10, 0.36); Lab 2 **0.24** (0.12, 0.40); Lab 3 **0.19** (0.09, 0.30); Lab 4 **0.18** (0.10, 0.28); Lab 5 **0.20** (0.11, 0.32).

  Systematic between-lab bias, Phase 1: "The averaged ratio of Lab 2 IC50s to the group averages for Phase 1 drugs is 4.5 (3.4 to 5.6)."
- **(C) Internal consistency check:** 2 × 1.96 × 0.18 = 0.706 ≈ the reported 0.69 interval; antilog₁₀(0.69) = 4.9. The 0.18 is therefore an SD **on the log₁₀ (pIC50) scale**, and the paper's own arithmetic confirms it.
- **(I) Caveat:** 0.18 is the residual **after** removing both drug and laboratory effects, under a standardized protocol, single technique, blinded. It is a **best-case lower bound**, not the noise in a heterogeneous literature dataset. Pooled across labs the spread is ~0.7 log units.

#### 1b. Kramer et al. 2020 — cross-site/cross-platform automated patch clamp (CiPA-associated)

- **Citation:** Kramer J, Himmel HM, Lindqvist A, et al. "Cross-site and cross-platform variability of automated patch clamp assessments of drug effects on human cardiac currents in recombinant cells." *Scientific Reports* 2020;10:5627. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-020-62344-w. PMC7101356.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. Open access.
- **Measured:** hERG, hCav1.2, peak and late hNav1.5; **12 blinded CiPA drugs**, **17 sites** (16 contributed hERG), **5 APC platforms** (SyncroPatch, Patchliner, QPatch, IonWorks Barracuda, IonFlux).
- **Error figures for hERG (R):**
  > "A wide range of values is discernable across compounds from larger datasets (n = 13–15), with 25/75 percentiles ranging from 1.02 log units (10.4-fold) for dofetilide to 0.22 log units (1.7-fold) for quinidine."

  > "For example, a repeatability coefficient of 3.7 for platform p3 means that typical measurements of hERG IC50 done with p3 were within 3.7-fold. Greater repeatability coefficients are evident for platforms 1, 2, and 4, with platform 1 providing a coefficient of 9.7 divergent IC50 measures within nearly 10-fold of each other."

  > "Within-platform variance predominated for 6 of 12 drugs (cisapride, mexiletine, ondansetron, quinidine, sotalol, and verapamil), while between-platform variance dominated for chlorpromazine, diltiazem, and terfenadine."
- **(C) IQR → SD conversion** (assuming approximate normality on log scale, SD = IQR/1.349): dofetilide **SD ≈ 0.76 log units**; quinidine **SD ≈ 0.16 log units**. Per-drug IQRs for the remaining 10 drugs are in Fig. 1 only and were **NOT VERIFIED** — I could not extract per-drug numbers from the figure, so no median across drugs is available.

### 2. KRAMER et al. 2012 AND ION-CHANNEL / hERG BREAKOUT

- **Citation:** Kramer C, Kalliokoski T, Gedeck P, Vulpetti A. "The Experimental Uncertainty of Heterogeneous Public Ki Data." *J Med Chem* 2012;55(11):5165–5173. DOI: 10.1021/jm300131x. PMID 22643060.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES.
- **Verbatim (R, full abstract via Europe PMC):**
  > "The experimental uncertainty is estimated to yield a mean error of 0.44 pK(i) units, a standard deviation of 0.54 pK(i) units, and a median error of 0.34 pK(i) units. The maximum possible squared Pearson correlation coefficient (R(2)) on large data sets is estimated to be 0.81."
- **Ion-channel / hERG breakout: NONE FOUND.** I checked Kramer 2012, Kalliokoski 2013, and Landrum & Riniker 2024. **(R)** Landrum & Riniker's only hERG mention is "a common target like hERG has 2020 assays" — no hERG-specific error statistic. **(I)** No paper located that partitions the 0.54 figure by target class.

**Companion (IC50 rather than Ki):**
- **Citation:** Kalliokoski T, Kramer C, Vulpetti A, Gedeck P. "Comparability of Mixed IC50 Data – A Statistical Analysis." *PLoS ONE* 2013;8(4):e61007. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0061007. PMC3628986.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. Open access.
- **(R):** σ_pIC50 = **0.68**; MUE_pIC50 = **0.55**; median UE = **0.43**; "Roughly 70% of all ΔpIC50's are smaller than one log unit."; raw R² = 0.40, R² = 0.53 after excluding ΔpIC50 ≥ 2.5. "the variability of heterogeneous IC50 data is roughly 25% worse than that of Ki data."
- **No hERG/ion-channel breakout (R).** The only per-target detail given is celecoxib/COX-2.

### 3. SAME-COMPOUND hERG VALUES ACROSS PUBLICATIONS / DATABASES

#### 3a. Sato et al. 2018 — THE ONLY hERG-SPECIFIC CROSS-SOURCE DISAGREEMENT NUMBER FOUND

- **Citation:** Sato T, Yuki H, Ogura K, Honma T. "Construction of an integrated database for hERG blocking small molecules." *PLoS ONE* 2018;13(7):e0199348. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0199348. PMC6034787.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. Open access.
- **Verbatim (R):**
  > "coefficient of determination and the root mean square deviation between the pIC50 values measured by binding assays and electrostatic assays were 0.517 and 0.737, respectively"

  (n = **209 compounds** measured by both assay formats; "119 compounds showed higher potencies in electrostatic assays"; mean IC50 2.41 µM binding vs 1.64 µM electrophysiology.)
  > "144 compounds showed consistent IC50 values with less than one order of magnitude differences between the maximum and minimum results"

  (out of **263 compounds with ≥3 reported IC50 values**; "47 compounds recorded more than 100-fold differences between the maximum and minimum IC50 values"; "224 out of the 263 compounds" had <10-fold difference between 25th and 75th percentiles.)
  > "assay entries that were at least 10-fold higher or lower than the mean value of the compound were defined as outliers"
- **(C)** RMSD 0.737 log units between two assay formats. If both formats carry equal independent error, the per-assay SD is 0.737/√2 = **0.52 log units** — which lands almost exactly on Kramer 2012's 0.54.
- **(I)** This is the closest thing in the literature to a direct hERG-specific "combining sources" noise estimate, and it is **cross-format** (binding vs electrophysiology), so it includes a method bias term, not purely random error.

#### 3b. Landrum & Riniker 2024 — the general ChEMBL cross-assay treatment (Ki relevant to this dataset)

- **Citation:** Landrum GA, Riniker S. "Combining IC50 or Ki Values from Different Sources Is a Source of Significant Noise." *J Chem Inf Model* 2024;64(5):1560–1567. DOI: 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00049. PMID 38394344. PMC10934815.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. Open access.
- **(R) Ki results (directly relevant — the user's endpoint is Ki):**
  - Minimal curation: MAE **0.52**; "67% of the Δpchembl values are greater than 0.3, and 30% are greater than 1.0" (7,734 compounds, 587 assays)
  - Maximal curation: MAE **0.47**; "69% of the Δpchembl values are greater than 0.3, and 32% are greater than 1.0" (2,434 compounds, 282 assays); 0.45 after pruning problem assays
  - **(R)** From the abstract: "Surprisingly, our analysis shows similar amounts of noise when combining data from different literature Kᵢ assays."
- **(R) IC50 results:** minimal MAE 0.50, "almost 65% of the points differ by more than 0.3 log units, 27% differ by more than one log unit… Kendall's τ is only 0.51"; maximal MAE 0.27, "48% of the points differ by more than 0.3 log units, 13% by more than one log unit, and Kendall's τ is 0.71."
- **(R) Noise floor statement:** "noise in the training data for the models sets an upper limit on the accuracy that can be expected"
- **NOTE (R):** the paper reports **MAE, not SD**. Do not quote an SD from this paper.

### 4. MAXIMUM ACHIEVABLE ACCURACY OF hERG MODELS

#### 4a. Lanevskij et al. 2022 — hERG-specific performance ceiling

- **Citation:** Lanevskij K, Didziapetris R, Sazonovas A. "Physicochemical QSAR analysis of hERG inhibition revisited: towards a quantitative potency prediction." *J Comput Aided Mol Des* 2022;36(12):837–849. DOI: 10.1007/s10822-022-00483-0. PMID 36305984.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. **PAYWALLED — abstract only** (numbers below are from the abstract).
- **Verbatim (R):**
  > "The new model achieved a similar level of accuracy of discerning hERG blockers from non-blockers at 10 µM threshold, which can be conceived as close to the performance ceiling for methods aiming to describe only non-specific ligand interactions with hERG."

  > "pIC50 from patch-clamp measurements can be predicted with R² ≈ 0.4 and MAE < 0.5, which enables ligand ranking according to their expected potency levels."

  (Database: "almost 9400 molecules", expanded from ~6700.)
- **(I)** This is an achieved-performance statement plus an assertion of a ceiling; it is **not** an assay-error-derived noise-floor derivation. Do not present it as an experimental-error figure.

#### 4b. Kolmar & Grulke 2021 — general noise-floor theory (NOT hERG-specific)

- **Citation:** Kolmar SS, Grulke CM. "The effect of noise on the predictive limit of QSAR models." *J Cheminform* 2021;13:92. DOI: 10.1186/s13321-021-00571-7. PMC8613965.
- **Peer-reviewed:** YES. Open access.
- **(R)** Cites the ChEMBL pIC50 average SD of 0.68 log units; added 1.1 log units of simulated noise to a BACE pIC50 set, described as "1.6 times the average standard deviation reported in ChemBL."
- **(R) Key claim, relevant to your noise-injection framing:** "it is often assumed in the field of QSAR that models cannot produce predictions which are more accurate than their training data" — the paper shows that under Gaussian error, models can predict *true* values more accurately than the noisy labels. **No hERG-specific number.**
- **(I)** This directly contradicts Kramer 2012's "models that yield errors smaller than the experimental uncertainty are necessarily overtrained." Useful tension to cite if your paper argues about noise ceilings.

### 5. CONTEXT CHECK — THE "INTER-ASSAY SD > 1.0 LOG UNIT" FILTER

**Finding: I could NOT locate any peer-reviewed source that establishes SD ≤ 1.0 log unit as standard practice, and no publication by Landrum uses it as a curation filter. This attribution appears to be NOT VERIFIED.**

What Landrum & Riniker actually do with 1.0 (R):
> "*f* > 1.0: fraction of the pairs where the difference is more than one log unit. This is an arbitrary limit for a truly meaningful activity difference."

That is a **reporting statistic on pairwise differences**, self-described as arbitrary, not a filter threshold, and not a standard deviation. **(I)** If the user's protocol is attributed to Landrum, the attribution should be softened to "following the spirit of Landrum & Riniker's maximal-curation argument" rather than stated as their protocol.

**Published hERG curation thresholds that DO exist (all peer-reviewed):**

| Source | Threshold used | Verbatim |
|---|---|---|
| Delre et al. 2022, *Front Pharmacol* 13:951083, DOI 10.3389/fphar.2022.951083 | **σ > 2** log units = outlier | "duplicates were aggregated in unique entries and the standard deviation (σ) related to the pIC50 values was computed. Also, 15 compounds were considered outliers (σ > 2) while for all of the others, the average pIC50 value was considered." (7,963 compounds) |
| Sato et al. 2018, *PLoS ONE* 13:e0199348 | **±10-fold from compound mean** = outlier (≈ ±1.0 log unit, per-entry not per-SD) | "assay entries that were at least 10-fold higher or lower than the mean value of the compound were defined as outliers" |
| Kalliokoski et al. 2013, *PLoS ONE* 8:e61007 | **ΔpIC50 ≥ 2.5** = probable error | R² rises 0.40 → 0.53 on exclusion |

**(I) The user's SD ≤ 1.0 filter is therefore stricter than Delre (σ ≤ 2) and roughly comparable to Sato's 10-fold rule, but it is not a canonical threshold and no paper defends it.**

#### What the retained noise floor actually is — (C), NOT published

**No published treatment of the retained-disagreement question was found.** The following is my own arithmetic, clearly labelled:

1. **(C) For a compound with exactly two measurements**, the sample SD (n=2, ddof=1) is |Δ|/√2. So a filter of SD ≤ 1.0 retains pairs differing by up to **√2 = 1.41 log units**, i.e. a **26-fold** potency discrepancy. This is a much looser filter than "1 log unit" sounds. Since ChEMBL duplicate sets are dominated by n=2 and n=3, this is the operative case for most of the 1,482 compounds.
2. **(C) Expected retained disagreement.** From Landrum & Riniker's Ki numbers, ~30–32% of same-target Ki pairs already differ by more than 1.0 log unit *before* any SD filter. A SD ≤ 1.0 filter removes only the pairs beyond 1.41 log units — a subset of that 30%. The retained mean absolute disagreement will therefore still be close to their reported **MAE ≈ 0.47–0.52 log units**, and the retained SD close to Kramer 2012's **0.54 pKi units**. Filtering at SD ≤ 1.0 trims the tail; it does not move the centre of the error distribution.
3. **(I) Implication for the paper:** the curated hERG Ki labels should be assumed to carry roughly **0.5 log units of SD** of irreducible label noise, essentially the unfiltered ChEMBL figure. The filter buys outlier removal, not a materially lower noise floor.

## BOTTOM LINE — MOST DEFENSIBLE NUMBER

**For the user's dataset (hERG Ki from ChEMBL, heterogeneous literature sources):**

> **σ = 0.54 log units (pKi standard deviation).**
> Kramer C, Kalliokoski T, Gedeck P, Vulpetti A. *J Med Chem* 2012;55(11):5165–5173. DOI: 10.1021/jm300131x.
> "The experimental uncertainty is estimated to yield a mean error of 0.44 pK(i) units, a standard deviation of 0.54 pK(i) units, and a median error of 0.34 pK(i) units."

**Status: STAND-IN, clearly labelled.** This is a ChEMBL-wide heterogeneous-Ki figure, **not hERG-specific**. No paper breaks it out by target or target class — I checked Kramer 2012, Kalliokoski 2013 and Landrum & Riniker 2024 and none does.

**Why it is nonetheless the right number to use here (I):**
- It is a **Ki** figure and the dataset is Ki — the endpoint matches exactly.
- It is an **SD**, which is what a Gaussian noise-injection study needs (Landrum & Riniker report only MAE and percentages; Kalliokoski's 0.68 is IC50, not Ki).
- It is corroborated by two independent routes: Landrum & Riniker 2024 Ki MAE 0.47–0.52 (peer-reviewed, ChEMBL, same-target cross-assay), and **(C)** Sato 2018's hERG-specific cross-format RMSD of 0.737 → per-assay SD 0.52.

**hERG-specific bracketing to quote alongside it (both peer-reviewed):**

| Regime | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Best case — standardized protocol, single technique, drug and lab effects removed | **SD = 0.18 pIC50** (residual τ); within-experiment median SD 0.18–0.24 | Alvarez Baron 2025, *Sci Rep* 15:29995, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-15761-8 |
| Realistic multi-lab, standardized protocol | **~5× IC50 spread ≈ 0.7 log units** (2.5–97.5 pct) | same |
| Cross-platform automated patch clamp, per drug | IQR **0.22 to 1.02 log units** (→ **(C)** SD 0.16 to 0.76) | Kramer J 2020, *Sci Rep* 10:5627, DOI 10.1038/s41598-020-62344-w |
| hERG cross-assay-format, literature | **RMSD 0.737 log units**, n=209 | Sato 2018, *PLoS ONE* 13:e0199348 |
| Heterogeneous literature Ki, all targets | **SD 0.54 pKi** | Kramer C 2012, *J Med Chem* 55:5165 |

**(I) Recommended framing for the paper:** "σ = 0.6 corresponds to roughly one unit of real hERG measurement error" is well supported. 0.54 (Kramer 2012, Ki, general) sits between the hERG multi-lab pooled spread (~0.7) and the hERG cross-format estimate (0.52 computed / 0.737 RMSD), and above the best-case standardized-protocol residual (0.18). Stating the range 0.5–0.7 log units with Kramer 2012 as the point estimate and Alvarez Baron 2025 as the hERG-specific anchor is defensible on peer-reviewed evidence alone.
