# Distribution of experimental error in bioactivity / ADME data

**Bottom line up front:** the published evidence says the error is **clearly NOT Gaussian — it is leptokurtic (sharp peak, fat tails)**. The best single characterisation supported by published data is a **Student-t with ν ≈ 4–6, or a Laplace**, not a normal. Injecting pure Gaussian noise is unrealistic in a specific, quantifiable way: it under-produces large errors by ~2× at 1 log unit, ~8× at 3 log units, and ~60× at 4 log units.

Every number below is tagged **[READ]** (taken verbatim from a source), **[COMPUTED]** (my arithmetic on published numbers), or **[INFERENCE]**.

### Figure 2 bin heights → my own tail fit **[COMPUTED]**
Reading the 1-log bin fractions off Figure 2 (±~0.005 by eye): [0,1) 0.68, [1,2) 0.23, [2,3) 0.068, [3,4) 0.018, [4,5) 0.004 (sums to 1.000). Survival function: P(|Δ|>1) = 0.32, P(>2) = 0.090, P(>3) = 0.022, P(>4) = 0.004.

| model, scale fitted to P(>1)=0.32 | P(>2) | P(>3) | P(>4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **observed** | 0.090 | 0.022 | 0.0040 |
| Gaussian, σ_Δ = 1.006 | 0.047 (**obs/pred 1.9×**) | 0.0029 (**7.7×**) | 0.00007 (**58×**) |

**[COMPUTED]** The empirical tail lies **between Gaussian and Laplace, much closer to Laplace**; a Student-t with **ν ≈ 4–6** brackets it. A Gaussian is decisively excluded.

**[COMPUTED]** The maximum, ΔpIC50 = 7.7 with fitted σ_Δ = 0.86, is **8.95σ**. Gaussian expected count in 16,844 pairs = **5.8 × 10⁻¹⁵** — i.e. ~14 orders of magnitude too improbable. A Laplace at the same σ predicts 0.05 such pairs; a Student-t with ν = 4 predicts 3.8; ν = 5 predicts 1.4.

**[COMPUTED]** Truncation inflation: crude moment estimate from the Figure-2 bins (midpoint approximation) gives SD_Δ ≈ 1.19 → per-measurement σ ≈ **0.84**, versus the paper's headline **0.68** obtained on data truncated at 2.5. **The published "0.68" is a core-only number; the untruncated SD is ~20–25% larger.**

For a symmetric distribution, SD/MedUE is a kurtosis diagnostic. Gaussian = 1.4826; Laplace = 2.0403.

| source | SD | MUE | MedUE | SD/MUE (Gauss 1.2533) | SD/MedUE (Gauss 1.4826) | implied Student-t ν |

## 3. Krüger & Overington (2012) — explicit non-normality test and an explicit Laplace fit

**Citation:** Krüger FA, Overington JP. "Global Analysis of Small Molecule Binding to Related Protein Targets." *PLoS Comput Biol* 2012;8(1):e1002333. DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002333. Open access; full PDF and Figure 1 read.

**Sample sizes [READ]:** 2,782 human–rat ortholog compound instances over 151 ortholog pairs; 41,733 paralog comparisons; and the inter-assay control: "this distribution was constructed as a composite of **1.500 randomly picked inter-assay differences for human targets and 1.500 randomly picked inter-assay differences for rat targets**." — **this is the n = 3,000 your slide deck refers to. Verified.**

**Non-normality, verbatim [READ]:**
> "The density distribution of differences in bioactivity has a central peak at 0 and **is non-normal as established by Anderson-Darling test [24] (p<2e-16)**."

**Laplace fit, verbatim [READ]:**
> "Both distributions can be approximately described by a **Laplace distribution**. Notably, the scale parameter b, which describes the spread of the distribution, is **b = 0.7 for the paralogs and b = 1.3 for human-to-rat orthologs**."

**⚠ IMPORTANT CAVEAT — do not over-claim this.** Both the Anderson-Darling test and the Laplace fit are applied to the **ortholog and paralog** difference distributions (biological difference), **not** to the pure inter-assay error distribution. What the paper does say about the inter-assay distribution [READ]: "The resulting distribution closely resembles the distribution for orthologs." **The paper does not quote a Laplace scale parameter for the inter-assay distribution itself.** [INFERENCE] By the authors' own "closely resembles" statement, the inter-assay error distribution is approximately Laplace with b of order 1, but they never assert this directly.

**Tail extent from Figure 1E (the inter-assay panel), read from the published image [READ from figure]:** a sharp cusp-like central peak at density ≈ 0.55 with visible density out to **approximately ±4 log units in δ(pKi)**, and a visible shoulder near ±3. **This confirms the "tails to ±3–4 log units" claim in your slide deck.** [COMPUTED] A Gaussian with peak density 0.55 has σ = 0.725, under which the density at |Δ| = 3 would be ~1×10⁻⁴ — i.e. invisible on that axis. The observed shape is Laplace/Student-t-like, not Gaussian.

**Other reported statistics [READ]:** Pearson r = 0.71 (p<2e-16) between human and rat pKi; Levene's test on ortholog vs paralog variance p<2.2e-16. All statistics done in R.

## 4. Landrum & Riniker (2024) — the best modern replication, with explicit outlier fractions

**Citation:** Landrum GA, Riniker S. "Combining IC50 or Ki Values from Different Sources Is a Source of Significant Noise." *J Chem Inf Model* 2024;64(5):1560–1567. DOI 10.1021/acs.jcim.4c00049. Open access (PMC10934815); full text read. Data/code: github.com/rinikerlab/overlapping_assays.

**Verbatim [READ]:**
> "IC50 assays selected using minimal curation settings have poor agreement with each other: **almost 65% of the points differ by more than 0.3 log units, 27% differ by more than one log unit**, and the correlation between the assays, as measured by Kendall's τ, is only 0.51. Requiring that most of the assay metadata in ChEMBL matches ("maximal curation") … improves the situation (**48% of the points differ by more than 0.3 log units, 13% by more than one log unit**, and Kendall's τ is 0.71) **at the expense of having smaller data sets**."

> "The noise level for Ki is comparable: **67% of the Δpchembl values are greater than 0.3, and 30% are greater than 1.0**."

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IC50 | only activity | 1358 | 38,022 | 0.31 | 0.51 | 0.50 | 0.64 | 0.27 |
| IC50 | maximal | 26 | 340 | 0.63 | 0.71 | 0.27 | 0.48 | 0.13 |
| IC50 large | only activity | 1599 | 50,385 | 0.32 | 0.51 | 0.51 | 0.65 | 0.28 |

| dataset | MAE | σ_G | P(>0.3) pred vs obs | P(>1.0) pred vs obs | **excess at 1 log** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IC50 activity-only | 0.50 | 0.627 | 0.632 vs 0.64 ✓ | 0.111 vs 0.27 | **2.44×** |

**[INFERENCE]** This is the cleanest statement available: a Gaussian calibrated to the *typical* error reproduces the shoulder at 0.3 log units almost exactly but **under-produces errors beyond 1 log unit by a factor of 2.4** in raw ChEMBL data, and by **>40×** after aggressive curation (curation removes the bulk, not the tail — the surviving distribution is *more* leptokurtic, not less).

Landrum & Riniker also document a concrete tail mechanism [READ]: 32 carbonic-anhydrase-I Ki assays sharing a corresponding author with internally inconsistent overlapping results; removing 239 such assays eliminated "most of the outliers." They state: "Because this is almost certainly **artificial variability and not just experimental noise**, we removed all data from assays that have more than ten compounds in common."

# Direct answers

## A) Is there published evidence that assay error is heavier-tailed than Gaussian?

**Yes, and it is strong.** In descending order of directness:

1. **Krüger & Overington 2012 [READ]** — the only paper that runs a formal normality test on a bioactivity-difference distribution: "**non-normal as established by Anderson-Darling test (p<2e-16)**" on n = 2,782, and fits a **Laplace** distribution (b = 0.7 / 1.3) to the affinity-difference densities. Caveat as noted: the test and fit are on ortholog/paralog differences, with the n = 3,000 inter-assay distribution described as "closely resembl[ing]" them. Figure 1E shows the inter-assay tails reaching ±4 log units.

2. **Kalliokoski 2013 [READ]** — the authors never fit a Gaussian to the whole distribution. They fit it only to the truncated core and the fitted σ **grows monotonically with the truncation point** (0.80 → 0.84 → 0.86). Max ΔpIC50 = 7.7 log units in 16,844 pairs. Their own Figure 5 shows the empirical density above the fitted Gaussian at both the peak and the wings. **[INFERENCE]** A truly Gaussian core would give a truncation-invariant σ; a rising σ means the outer bands hold more mass than a normal allows.

3. **Landrum & Riniker 2024 [READ + COMPUTED]** — 27% (IC50) / 30% (Ki) of duplicate pairs differ by >1 log unit against MAE 0.50/0.52. **A Gaussian with the same MAE predicts 11–12.5%. Observed/predicted = 2.4×.** This is the cleanest and most quotable single statement of non-Gaussianity, and it is a 2024 replication on ChEMBL32.

**Strongest quantitative statement you can make [COMPUTED, from Kalliokoski Fig. 2 bins]:** relative to a Gaussian matched at the 1-log-unit point, the observed pair-difference distribution has **1.9× the mass beyond 2 log units, 7.7× beyond 3, and 58× beyond 4**.

**Important framing caveat.** Kalliokoski's manual audit (Table 2) and Landrum's carbonic-anhydrase case show the far tail is **largely provenance error** — unit transcription, wrong target, wrong assay type, retracted sources — not measurement imprecision. **[INFERENCE]** This *strengthens* the case for your paper rather than weakening it: for anyone training a QSAR model on ChEMBL-derived labels, both components are label noise and both are present. But state it explicitly: the heavy tail is a **contaminated-label** phenomenon, and the pure within-assay component (σ ≈ 0.17–0.29, see below) is much smaller and probably closer to Gaussian.

**The single most quotable sentence for a paper:** *roughly one in four to one in three duplicate potency measurements in ChEMBL disagree by more than a full log unit — about 2.4× more than a Gaussian with the same mean absolute error would produce.*

## C) Has anyone fitted a distribution to assay error?

| who | family fitted | parameters | scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalliokoski 2013 [READ] | **Gaussian**, but only to the **truncated core** (|Δ| ∈ [0.05, T], T = 1.5/2.0/2.5) | σ_Δ(pIC50) = 0.80 / 0.84 / 0.86; σ_Δ(pKi) = 0.66 / 0.68 / 0.68; Fig 5 σ_Δ = 0.87 and 0.69 | ChEMBL14 pairs |
| Kramer 2012 [NOT VERIFIED] | same truncated-Gaussian method (per Kalliokoski's description) | σ = 0.54, MUE = 0.44, MedUE = 0.34 per measurement | ChEMBL12 pKi pairs |
| Krüger & Overington 2012 [READ] | **Laplace** | **b = 0.7** (paralogs), **b = 1.3** (human–rat orthologs) | affinity-difference densities; NOT the pure inter-assay distribution |
| Cortés-Ciriano & Bender 2016 [READ] | truncated at ΔpIC50 < 2.5 | σE = 0.59–0.77 (cytotoxicity), 0.47–0.48 (pKi), 0.57–0.61 (pIC50) | ChEMBL19 |

**Nobody has published a Student-t or mixture-of-normals fit to bioactivity assay error.** That is a genuine gap and an opportunity for your paper.

**My own fits, offered as [COMPUTED], not as literature:**
- To the four tail points read off Kalliokoski Fig. 2: **Student-t, ν ≈ 4.3, scale 0.89** on the pair-difference scale → **scale ≈ 0.63 per measurement**. Laplace with b = 0.88 (pair scale) is nearly as good; Gaussian is not.
- Moment-ratio (SD/MedUE) on the *truncated core* of three independent datasets: **ν ≈ 8–16 in all three** (Kalliokoski pIC50, Kramer pKi, Cortés-Ciriano cytotoxicity).
- **[INFERENCE]** A defensible two-part characterisation: a mildly leptokurtic core (ν ≈ 10) plus a contamination tail from curation errors; the *combined* distribution behaves like **Student-t with ν ≈ 4–6**. If you want a single injectable noise model, **Student-t with ν = 4 or ν = 5, scaled to match the published σ**, is defensible and directly motivated by the data above. A **Laplace** is the simplest defensible alternative and is the only heavy-tailed family with a published precedent (Krüger & Overington).

# What this means for your noise-injection design

**[INFERENCE throughout this section.]**

1. **Gaussian injection is defensible only as a baseline, and you can now say precisely why it is unrealistic.** Cortés-Ciriano 2015 and Kolmar & Grulke 2021 both used Gaussian; Kolmar & Grulke explicitly concede the condition "is certainly not representative of every real-world data situation." You can cite that concession and then close the gap.

2. **Add a heavy-tailed strategy.** The most defensible choices, in order:
   - **Student-t with ν = 4** (or ν = 5), scale set so the SD matches the Gaussian arm — justified by my fit to Kalliokoski Fig. 2 and bracketed by the ν ≈ 8–16 core estimates.
   - **Laplace** — the only heavy-tailed family with a direct published precedent for this data type (Krüger & Overington 2012, b = 0.7/1.3).
   - **Contaminated normal** (e.g. 95% N(0, σ) + 5% N(0, 5σ)) — mechanistically the most honest, since Kalliokoski Table 2 and Landrum's carbonic-anhydrase case show the tail is discrete curation error, not smooth measurement noise. But no one has published fitted parameters, so you would have to fit and justify them yourself.

3. **Your existing `outlier` strategy may already be the right thing** — it is a contamination model. The gap in the paper is not the strategy, it is the *justification*: right now the heavy-tailed arms have no literature anchor. The numbers in this report supply one.

4. **A calibration note on σ.** `MEMORY.md` says "σ=0.6 ≈ one unit of real experimental error." That is well supported for **pIC50** (0.68 truncated, ~0.84 untruncated) and slightly generous for **pKi** (0.54). It is **not** supported for logD or Caco-2 — those anchors are unverified.

5. **One correction to flag:** `MEMORY.md` lists "hERG pKi 0.54." **0.54 is Kramer 2012's general heterogeneous-public-pKi figure, not a hERG-specific number.** I found no hERG-specific assay-error source. Either re-attribute it as generic pKi or mark it NOT VERIFIED.
