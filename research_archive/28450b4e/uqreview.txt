7. Calibration
Guo et al. 2017 - "On calibration of modern neural networks" ✓
Full Citation: Guo, C., Pleiss, G., Sun, Y., & Weinberger, K. Q. (2017). On Calibration of Modern Neural
Networks. Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML), Sydney,
Australia, PMLR 70, pages 1321-1330. ArXiv: 1706.04599
Direct Quotes on Calibration Metrics:
Perfect Calibration: "More formally, we define perfect calibration as: P(Ŷ = Y | P̂ = p) = p, ∀p ∈ [0, 1]
where the probability is over the joint distribution."
Expected Calibration Error (ECE): "Expected Calibration Error (Naeini et al., 2015) – or ECE –
approximates (2) by partitioning predictions into M equally-spaced bins (similar to the reliability diagrams)
and taking a weighted average of the bins' accuracy/confidence difference. More precisely,

ECE = ∑(M, m=1) |Bm|/n |acc(Bm) − conf(Bm)|
where n is the number of samples. The difference between acc and conf for a given bin represents the
calibration gap."
Maximum Calibration Error (MCE): "The Maximum Calibration Error (Naeini et al., 2015) – or MCE –
estimates an upper bound of this deviation. Similarly to ECE, this approximation involves binning:
MCE = max(m∈{1,...,M}) |acc(Bm) − conf(Bm)|
In reliability diagrams, MCE measures the largest calibration gap (red bars) across all bins, whereas ECE
measures a weighted average of all gaps. For perfectly calibrated classifiers, MCE and ECE both equal 0."
Temperature Scaling: "Temperature scaling, the simplest extension of Platt scaling, uses a single scalar
parameter T > 0 for all classes. Given the logit vector zi, the new confidence prediction is q̂ i = maxk
σSM(zi/T)(k). T is called the temperature, and it 'softens' the softmax (i.e. raises the output entropy) with T
> 1... T is optimized with respect to NLL on the validation set. Because the parameter T does not change the
maximum of the softmax function, the class prediction ŷ'i remains unchanged. In other words, temperature
scaling does not affect the model's accuracy."
Key Findings: "We discover that modern neural networks, unlike those from a decade ago, are poorly
calibrated. Through extensive experiments, we observe that depth, width, weight decay, and Batch
Normalization are important factors influencing calibration."
"Although increasing depth and width may reduce classification error, we observe that these increases
negatively affect model calibration... The ECE metric grows substantially with model capacity."
"Our most important discovery is the surprising effectiveness of temperature scaling despite its
remarkable simplicity. Temperature scaling outperforms all other methods on the vision tasks."
Verification: ICML 2017, pages 1321-1330. Cornell University authors. ArXiv 1706.04599.
