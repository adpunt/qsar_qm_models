## D) BASELINE R² — THE THRESHOLD ALREADY BIT, AND THE TABLE IS CENSORED

Distribution of `baseline_r2` in `table_validation_auc_full.csv`:

| dataset | n | mean | std | min | 25% | 50% | 75% | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ChEMBL-hERG-Ki | 153 | 0.513 | 0.066 | **0.309** | 0.472 | 0.518 | 0.550 | 0.635 |
| OpenADMET-Caco2_Efflux | 156 | 0.451 | 0.054 | **0.335** | 0.398 | 0.464 | 0.496 | 0.534 |
| OpenADMET-LogD | 156 | 0.762 | 0.061 | 0.567 | 0.747 | 0.773 | 0.791 | 0.866 |

Fraction below thresholds: **<0.3 → 0.000 on all three** (the file is pre-filtered, see below). **<0.4 → hERG 0.059, Caco-2 0.276, LogD 0.000. <0.5 → hERG 0.373, Caco-2 0.763, LogD 0.000.**

**The zero at 0.3 is not a result — it is the filter.** `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:435` sets `VALIDATION_BASELINE_THRESHOLD = 0.3` and line 1382 (`if baseline < VALIDATION_BASELINE_THRESHOLD: continue`) drops configurations before this CSV is written. Note also this is a **looser gate than QM9 uses**: `BASELINE_THRESHOLD = 0.6` at line 425. Configurations kept on hERG at R²=0.31 would have been excluded outright on QM9. The paper compares robustness across datasets that were quality-gated at different levels, and the figure caption near paper.tex:545 discloses only the 0.3 rule.

A second filter censors the metric itself: `VALIDATION_AUC_LOW, VALIDATION_AUC_HIGH = -0.5, 1.1` (line 439), NaN-ing extremes. 465 rows are written but only 149 hERG / 153 Caco-2 / 156 LogD have non-null auc_norm.

**Answer to the question as posed: yes, and the paper should say so — but the sharper case is Caco-2, not hERG.** On Caco-2 **76.3% of surviving configurations sit below R²=0.5** and 27.6% below 0.4, with a ceiling of 0.534. On hERG 37.3% are below 0.5, ceiling 0.635. LogD is genuinely healthy (min 0.567). Weakest cells (mean over the 6 strategies — an average): hERG `dnn` 0.383, Caco-2 `xgboost/continuous_pdv` 0.335, Caco-2 `ngboost/sns` 0.398.

Two consequences worth stating in the paper:
- auc_norm is a **retention fraction R²(σ)/R²(0)**, so at R²(0)≈0.34 the denominator is small and noisy, and the metric swings wildly — Caco-2 has the largest rep-to-rep ranges of any dataset (up to 0.440 auc_norm for xgboost/legacy across three reps).
- auc_norm and baseline R² are **not decoupled in practice**, contrary to the design rationale in the code comment at line 425: Spearman(auc_norm, baseline_r2) = **+0.330 (p=4.0e-5) on hERG**, **−0.423 (p=3.7e-8) on LogD**, +0.031 (p=0.71) on Caco-2. The sign reverses between datasets, which means "robustness" on these sets partly re-encodes how well the model fit in the first place, in opposite directions.

Side note (**uncertainty workstream, one line only**): `baseline_r2` is not constant across strategies within a configuration although σ=0 should be identical — max within-config spread **0.134** (ChEMBL-hERG-Ki / dnn / sns, 0.3090 → 0.4434). That is seed/run noise leaking into the σ=0 anchor, and it collides directly with D because the anchor is the auc_norm denominator.

## E) ESTIMATING THE PRE-EXISTING NOISE FLOOR

**Yes, there is a usable signal in the σ-curve shape, and it is one of the more publishable things in this data.** Injected noise is drawn as `Normal::new(0.0, target_sigma)` on the raw label (`rust/src/main.rs:207,213,235`), i.e. **σ is in absolute label units** — for LogD, log units. If the label already carries measurement error of SD σ₀, effective noise is √(σ₀²+σ²), so R²(σ) is **flat and quadratic near σ=0** rather than dropping linearly. On truly clean labels it should fall from the first step.

Computed on the legacy (Gaussian) curves in `results/validation_full/` — LogD + Caco-2, 22 configurations, no averaging across configurations:

Median first-step slope ΔR²/Δσ over σ 0→0.1: **LogD −0.005, Caco-2 −0.034.** Median second-step slope (0.1→0.2): **LogD −0.050, Caco-2 −0.548.** Median fifth-step (0.4→0.5): **LogD −0.174 for QRF/ECFP4-class configs, Caco-2 −1.011 to −1.862.** The curves are flat at the origin and accelerate — the quadrature signature. Concretely, `RF/PDV` on LogD reads 0.696, 0.695, 0.696, 0.683 at σ = 0.0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3 (`results/validation_full/openadmet_logd/RF_PDV_legacy.csv`): injecting a **0.2 log-unit** label error changes R² by 0.000. That is a directly quotable, medicinally meaningful statement.

Fitting 1/R² against σ² (linear if noise adds in quadrature) gives per-configuration effective-noise intercepts: median **σ₀_eff ≈ 1.94 (LogD)**, **0.588 (Caco-2)** in label units, fit quality R²=0.82–0.99. **Do not report σ₀_eff as an assay noise floor.** The intercept absorbs *all* irreducible error — model bias, split difficulty and assay noise together — so 1.94 log units is an upper bound, not an estimate, and it is far above published logD assay SDs. It is only usable as a bound.

What could be done, and what it needs:
1. **Direct assay-repeatability estimate.** ChEMBL holds multiple independent pKi records per compound-target pair, and OpenADMET reports replicate measurements. Compute the within-compound SD across replicates → σ₀ in the same units as injected σ. *Needs: the raw source label tables with per-measurement rows, which are not in this repo (`data/` holds only `QM9/` and `valid_qm9_indices.pth`).*
2. **Calibration by construction.** Re-run the QM9 pipeline with a known σ₀ pre-injected into the labels before the experiment starts, then match the curve shape to the external curves. This is identifiable because σ₀ is known. *Needs: one extra QM9 sweep — a normal ARC job.*
3. **Fix the anchor first.** Any curve-shape method depends on R²(0) being a clean single number; today it moves by up to 0.134 across strategy re-runs (D). *Needs: the σ=0 baseline computed once per configuration and reused, plus replicates.*
4. **The identifiability limit, stated plainly:** from a single R²-vs-σ curve you cannot separate assay noise from model error. Two curves at different training-set sizes, or an external σ₀ anchor, are required. Say this in the paper rather than the current line 551, which raises the noise floor and then drops it.

## F) THE ONE ACTIONABLE SENTENCE

**Candidate:** *"On log-scale ADME endpoints, adding up to about 0.2–0.3 log units of extra label error to your training set changes model quality essentially not at all — so a cheaper, noisier assay is worth using — but this holds only for LogD-like endpoints where the model already reaches R² ≈ 0.7, and it does not hold for Caco-2 efflux."*

**Is it supported? Partly — and the qualifier is doing real work.**

Supported: on LogD, `RF/PDV` legacy runs 0.696 → 0.695 → 0.696 → 0.683 over σ = 0 → 0.3 (`results/validation_full/openadmet_logd/RF_PDV_legacy.csv`); `QRF/PDV` 0.693 → 0.693 → 0.687 → 0.681; `RF/MHG-GNN-pretrained` 0.704 → 0.703 → 0.696 → 0.699. Across all LogD cells the strategy-to-strategy auc_norm range within a configuration has median **0.120** and the rep-to-rep range within (model, strategy) has median **0.020** — the endpoint is stable no matter how you slice it.

Not supported on the other two. The same within-configuration strategy range has median **0.496 on hERG** and **0.563 on Caco-2** (max 1.280 and 1.410). Worst-case auc_norm by model (minimum over rep × strategy, `table_validation_auc_full.csv`): hERG dnn −0.248, xgboost −0.247, qrf 0.163; Caco-2 xgboost −0.403, qrf −0.129, lgb 0.115. Fraction of cells reaching auc_norm ≥ 0.9: LogD ngboost 1.00 and svm 1.00, versus Caco-2 svm 0.22, ngboost 0.17, xgboost 0.05.

**The model-choice version of the sentence is weaker than the paper implies.** *"Pick NGBoost or SVM if you expect noisy labels"* is supported on LogD (ngboost 1.00 and svm 1.00 of cells at auc_norm ≥ 0.9, worst cases 0.963 and 0.910) and is the least-bad choice on hERG (svm 0.67, ngboost 0.54; worst cases 0.763 and 0.739, the only two models never going negative on any dataset). But on Caco-2 both fall to 0.22 / 0.17 and NGBoost's worst cell is 0.278. And **all of it rests on one seed per configuration** — with no replicates there is no way to say how much of any gap is run-to-run noise.

**Strongest fully-supported sentence, if only one is wanted:** *"Across three ADME endpoints, only NGBoost and SVM never suffered a catastrophic robustness failure under any of the six noise types (minimum auc_norm 0.278 and 0.507 respectively), while XGBoost and QRF went negative — i.e. worse than predicting the mean — on two of the three datasets."* Sources: minima table in F above, from `results/paper_figures_v2/table_validation_auc_full.csv`.
