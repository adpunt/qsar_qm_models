## σ = 0.6 FRAMING — VERIFIED, WITH ONE CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF

Verified in-repo at `DISCUSSION_TRACKER.md:125-128` and `REVISION_GUIDE.md:343-374`. Published assay-error SDs, same log units as the labels: hERG pKi **0.54** (Kramer 2012), pIC50 **0.68** (Kalliokoski 2013), hERG pIC50 RMSD **0.737** (Sato 2018), logD **MAE 0.48** (Niu 2024), Caco-2 log10 **~0.43** (Hayeshi 2008). Units check: injection is `y + σ·N(0,1)` in raw label units, all three validation labels are logs (`REVISION_GUIDE.md:347`).

**Correction:** the brief's "logD 0.27–0.62" is wrong. The 0.27 (Bruneau & McElroy 2006) **failed verification and is marked do-not-use** at `DISCUSSION_TRACKER.md:125`. The defensible band is **roughly 0.4–0.74, clustered near 0.5** (`REVISION_GUIDE.md:363`). σ=0.6 ≈ one unit of real experimental error stands.

**Two caveats that must ride with any σ=0.6 recommendation** (`REVISION_GUIDE.md:371-372`): (1) QM9 is DFT-computed — no experimental error exists, the anchoring does *not* transfer to the QM9 half of the paper; (2) public labels already carry ~0.54, so σ=0.6 *doubles* it (√(0.54²+0.6²)≈0.81) — phrase as "an additional unit", never "we add realistic noise to clean data".

### B2. Winner identity is near-invariant

Top model per (rep, strategy), 50 cells: **NGBoost wins 30 of them**; NGBoost's rank is **median 1, min 1, max 5** out of 5–11 models. Confirmed on validation: `deep_validation_model_robustness_by_strategy.csv` — SVM is top for outlier on Caco-2 and LogD, and for threshold on hERG-Ki and Caco-2.

### B3. **The regret number — this is the sentence to publish**

Suppose the practitioner assumes plain Gaussian noise and picks the model that is best under Gaussian. How much auc_norm does that cost them if the noise is actually one of the other five types?

| rep | model picked on Gaussian | **max regret across the other 5 strategies** |
|---|---|---|
| pdv | ngboost | **0.0011** |
| continuous_pdv | ngboost | 0.0027 |
| mol2vec | ngboost | 0.0050 |
| smiles | ngboost | 0.0052 |
| randomized_smiles | ngboost | 0.0075 |
| mhggnn | ngboost | 0.0114 |
| ecfp4 | ngboost | 0.0157 |
| sns | lgb | **0.0000** |
| morgan | dnn_bnn_full | **0.1195** ⚠ |

**For 8 of 9 representations the penalty for being wrong about your noise type is ≤ 0.016 auc_norm — well under 2 percentage points of retention.**

⚠ The morgan exception is a suspect cell, not a counterexample: `table2_supp_auc_all_reps.csv` row 57 (`dnn_bnn_full`, `morgan`) has Gaussian = **0.9297**, which is *higher than its own Outlier value* (0.9438 is normal; Gaussian above ~0.86 is not — every other model's Gaussian on morgan sits 0.778–0.849). It is also the single largest positive Gaussian interaction residual in the whole grid (+0.0785, section D). Flag this cell for a data check before it is used or cited.

**⇒ Publishable claim, fully supported:** *A practitioner who knows their labels are noisy but not how does not need to characterise the noise before choosing a model. Model rankings agree across all six noise mechanisms (Kendall's W = 0.912), and choosing as if the noise were plain Gaussian costs at most 0.016 in retained-R² fraction for 8 of 9 representations. The one qualification is outlier-dominated noise, where SVM overtakes NGBoost in 7 of 9 representations.*

**Caveat that must be stated:** stability is weaker on the real datasets than on QM9. `deep_validation_model_robustness_by_strategy.csv` gives per-dataset strategy-pair Spearman medians of **0.886 (hERG-Ki), 0.714 (LogD), 0.679 (Caco-2, min −0.214)**. "You don't need to know your noise type" is strong on QM9, moderate on real ADMET data.

## C) DOES REPRESENTATION MATTER, ONCE THE MODEL IS CHOSEN? — TESTED DIRECTLY

Computed from `table2_supp_auc_all_reps.csv` per strategy, never pooled across strategies.

| strategy | median range across **reps** (model fixed) | median range across **models** (rep fixed) | **ratio** | median SD reps | median SD models | SD ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian | 0.0250 | 0.1080 | **4.32×** | 0.0112 | 0.0374 | 3.34 |
| Threshold | 0.0436 | 0.1757 | **4.03×** | 0.0189 | 0.0670 | 3.55 |
| Outlier | 0.0079 | 0.0298 | **3.79×** | 0.0028 | 0.0100 | 3.54 |
| Value-Prop. | 0.0397 | 0.1405 | **3.54×** | 0.0176 | 0.0512 | 2.90 |
| Heteroscedastic | 0.0169 | 0.0546 | **3.22×** | 0.0069 | 0.0185 | 2.67 |
| Quantile | 0.0281 | 0.0795 | **2.83×** | 0.0131 | 0.0301 | 2.29 |

**Model spread exceeds representation spread in all six strategies without exception, by 2.8× to 4.3× (median range) or 2.3× to 3.5× (median SD).** This reproduces the ANOVA conclusion from the raw derived grid without going through η²: `table1_anova_summary.csv` Robust_Model_η² vs Robust_Rep_η² = 43.8 vs 5.2 (Gaussian), 54.7 vs 7.9 (Threshold), 52.5 vs 6.0 (Value-Prop.), 36.8 vs 4.4 (Quantile), 14.0 vs 0.68 (Hetero), 10.3 vs 0.22 (Outlier). Validation is even more lopsided: `table_validation_anova.csv` Model_η² 91.8/92.4/95.2 vs Rep_η² 4.2/0.9/0.2.

**But note the direction reverses for baseline performance.** In the same `table1_anova_summary.csv`, the *performance* columns show Interaction η² = 46.2–49.2% in every strategy, against Model 24.6–27.9% and Rep 21.2–23.5%. So: **interaction dominates how well you predict; model dominates how much of that you keep under noise.** These are two different claims and the paper currently blurs them.

**Practical statement:** representation is not irrelevant — it is worth a median 0.080 absolute R² at σ=0.6 (section A2), which is real. It is simply worth *less* than model choice, consistently, under every noise mechanism.

### D3. Named findings for the paper

1. **The interaction is almost entirely a neural-network phenomenon.** Every one of the six largest-magnitude consistent pairings involves MLP, DNN, or a BNN variant. Tree ensembles sit near zero everywhere — the one exception is **RF × PDV, consistently negative in all 6 strategies (mean −0.026)**, which directly contradicts `paper.tex:462` ("PDV stood out as having particularly strong robustness... RF and NN-β show the opposite pattern").
2. **The pairing the paper should name is MLP × mol2vec (+0.062) and its mirror MLP × SMILES (−0.065)** — the same architecture, swinging 0.127 in auc_norm purely on representation. That single pairing is the strongest empirical case for the interaction claim and it is not named anywhere in `paper.tex`.
3. **Interaction magnitude scales with strategy severity.** Residual SD: Outlier 0.0081 → Hetero 0.0119 → Quantile 0.0141 → Gaussian 0.0210 → Value-Prop. 0.0277 → **Threshold 0.0362**. The interaction is 4.5× larger under threshold noise than under outlier noise. Under outlier noise the interaction is essentially absent — matching `table1_anova_summary.csv` Robust_Interaction_η² = 5.86% for Outlier vs 22.58% for Threshold.
4. **PDV's story is model-conditional, not universal.** PDV is the best marginal representation for robustness (`deep_qm9_rep_robustness_by_strategy.csv`: pdv hetero 0.9169, outlier 0.9500, threshold 0.6208 — top or near-top in 5 of 6 strategies), *but* it is a positive interaction for the two neural nets and a negative interaction for RF. "PDVs are preferable when dealing with noisy data" (`paper.tex:462`) is only true if you name the model.

## E) COST — WHAT `paper.tex` SAYS, AND WHERE IT IS THIN

Every cost statement in `paper.tex` is qualitative. **There is no measured runtime anywhere in `results/` or `scripts/` — no `wall_time`, `train_time`, `fit_time`, or `elapsed` column exists in any results file.** Cost claims currently rest entirely on cited asymptotics.

| line | claim | status |
|---|---|---|
| `paper.tex:210` | NGBoost "scales like standard boosting in N but with larger constants… inverts N matrices of size p×p, adding O(Np³) cost per iteration… may limit scalability to larger datasets" (Duan 2020) | Cited asymptotic, no measurement |
| `paper.tex:216` | full-BNN = "one straightforward yet computationally expensive approach"; all BNN variants use "100 Monte Carlo forward passes at inference" | Qualitative; the 100 passes is a concrete inference-cost multiplier and should be stated as such |
| `paper.tex:466` | "Full transformations are more computationally expensive, making VBLL a potentially stronger approach… when working with larger data sets" | Qualitative |
| `paper.tex:567` | "the choice of both model and representation comes down to your problem, including… your compute limits" | Hand-wave; the paper's only closing cost statement |

**Where a recommendation needs a cost caveat, and the caveat is currently unwritten:**

1. **NGBoost is the #1 robustness recommendation and the paper's own text says it may not scale** (`paper.tex:210`). The whole study is N=10,000 (`paper.tex:193`). Any "use NGBoost" recommendation must carry: *validated at N=10,000; O(Np³) per iteration means this is untested at screening-library scale.*
2. **VBLL is recommended over full-BNN on cost grounds at `paper.tex:466`, but the robustness evidence does not support it.** `table3_wilcoxon_tests.csv:3` — `dnn vs dnn_vbll`, p = **0.252, not significant**, improvement only +0.0113 (n=24). The cheap option is also, for NN-α, the one with no demonstrated benefit. The paper recommends it anyway.
3. **The cost-free recommendation is missing.** SVM is top for Outlier in 7 of 9 reps and top on validation for outlier/threshold, and carries none of NGBoost's scaling warning — but `paper.tex` never pairs SVM's robustness with its cost profile. Note also `paper.tex:197` says SVM uses a Tanimoto kernel for binary reps, contradicting `paper.tex:262` ("the RBF was used across all representations"); the memory record confirms RBF throughout. Cost claims about SVM are unsafe until that contradiction is resolved.
4. **QRF is the clearest "don't pay for this" result and is under-sold.** `deep_validation_uncertainty_rfqrf.csv` — QRF is worse than RF on all three datasets: ΔAUC_norm −0.132 (hERG-Ki), −0.124 (Caco-2), −0.042 (LogD); `table3_wilcoxon_tests.csv:6` p=2.88e-11, improvement −0.0117. Extra machinery, strictly worse robustness.

## F) THE RECOMMENDATIONS THE DATA CAN CARRY

**R1 — Do not characterise your noise before choosing a model.** Model rankings are concordant across all six noise mechanisms at Kendall's W = **0.9121** (p = 3.55e-08) [`results/paper_figures_v2/table6_kendalls_w.txt:6`], and the penalty for choosing as though the noise were plain Gaussian is **≤ 0.0157 auc_norm for 8 of 9 representations** (pdv 0.0011, sns 0.0000, ecfp4 0.0157) [computed from `results/paper_figures_v2/table2_supp_auc_all_reps.csv`, all 81 rows, per-strategy, no averaging]. *Qualification:* on real ADMET data the median strategy-pair Spearman drops to 0.679 (Caco-2) / 0.714 (LogD) [`results/paper_figures_v2/deep_validation_model_robustness_by_strategy.csv`], so state this as strong on QM9, moderate externally.

**R2 — Spend your effort on the model, not the representation, but do not tell readers the representation is free.** Model choice is worth a **median 0.119 absolute R² at σ=0.6 (range 0.035–0.346)**; representation choice is worth a **median 0.080 (range 0.051–0.186)** [computed from `results/validation_full/{openadmet_logd,openadmet_caco2}/*.csv`, columns `sigma`/`r2`, 112 configs after the R²(0) ≥ 0.3 gate at `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:434`]. In retention terms the ratio is larger: model spread exceeds representation spread by **2.8×–4.3×** in every one of the six strategies [`results/paper_figures_v2/table2_supp_auc_all_reps.csv`].

**R3 — Never pick a model on retention alone; retention × baseline is the number that matters.** On LogD, NGBoost has the best retention (median auc_norm **0.982**) and the worst usable accuracy (baseline R² **0.706** → retained **0.693**), while the DNN has worse retention (**0.934**) and the best usable accuracy (baseline **0.866** → retained **0.809**) [`results/paper_figures_v2/table_validation_auc_full.csv`, columns `auc_norm` and `baseline_r2`, rows `dataset=OpenADMET-LogD`]. The ranking inverts.

**R4 — Avoid the specific pairings, not the factors.** MLP × SMILES underperforms its own marginals by **−0.065 auc_norm on average across all 6 strategies (−0.143 under threshold noise)**; MLP × mol2vec *over*performs by **+0.062 (+0.114 under threshold)** — a 0.127 swing on one architecture from representation alone [computed from `results/paper_figures_v2/table2_supp_auc_all_reps.csv`]. RF × PDV is negative in all six strategies (mean −0.026), which the paper currently claims the opposite of at `paper.tex:462`.

**R5 — Skip QRF.** Worse than RF on all three external datasets: ΔAUC_norm **−0.132 / −0.124 / −0.042** [`results/paper_figures_v2/deep_validation_uncertainty_rfqrf.csv`, rows 2–4], Wilcoxon p = **2.88e-11**, improvement **−0.0117** over n=44 [`results/paper_figures_v2/table3_wilcoxon_tests.csv:6`].

**NEARLY — "here is the cost of each recommendation."** No timing column exists in any results file. **Closing run:** add per-fit wall-clock capture to the training loop and re-run one representative sweep — a single (rep × 11 σ × 1 seed) pass over the 11 models is enough to state relative cost, and it is what turns `paper.tex:210`'s O(Np³) warning and `paper.tex:466`'s VBLL argument into evidence.

**NEARLY — "small datasets suffer most."** Only three external N values exist (5,039 / 2,161 / 1,482, `paper.tex:197`) and QM9 preliminary sizes 5k/10k/20k/30k are described qualitatively at `paper.tex:193` with no retained numbers.
