# auc_norm stress-test

## 0. The definition and the code

**Paper** — `/Users/apunt/repos/qsar_qm_models/paper.tex:242-247`:
> "we recorded $R^2(\sigma)$ and normalised it by the clean-label value to obtain the retention ratio $R^2(\sigma)/R^2(0)$. Our robustness metric is the normalised area under this retention curve, $\text{AUC}_{\text{norm}} = \frac{1}{\sigma_{\max}} \int_0^{\sigma_{\max}} \frac{R^2(\sigma)}{R^2(0)} d\sigma$ … $\text{AUC}_{\text{norm}}$ falls on $[0,1]$ … Since each curve is normalized by its own clean-label performance, $\text{AUC}_{\text{norm}}$ is not confounded by baseline accuracy. Configurations with baseline R$^2 < 0.3$ were excluded…"

**Code** — `/Users/apunt/repos/qsar_qm_models/scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1791-1800`:
```python
def _retention_auc_norm(sig, r2, base):
    """...HIGHER = more robust (≈[0,1]); decoupled from baseline performance..."""
    ret = r2 / base
    srange = sig.max() - sig.min()
    return float(trapezoid(ret, sig) / srange) if srange > 0 else np.nan
```
There is **no guard on the sign or magnitude of `base`**, and no clipping of `ret`. The `[0,1]` range asserted at `paper.tex:247` is not enforced anywhere and is not true of the outputs (see C).

Note the pre-step at `generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1818-1819` — `avg_group = group.groupby('sigma')['r2'].mean()` — **iterations are averaged before the ratio is taken**. auc_norm is therefore a ratio-of-means, not a mean-of-ratios, and the 10-replicate spread is destroyed before the metric exists. This hides that at low baseline the per-replicate auc_norm values are wildly dispersed (see A3).

## A) Arithmetic of dividing by R²(0)

**A1 — the metric is scale-free in R², so it is blind to absolute usefulness.** Two curves that are scalar multiples of each other get *identical* auc_norm. Verified numerically (σ = 0.0…1.0, step 0.1, trapezoid, same rule as the code):

| config | R²(0) | R²(0.6) | R²(1.0) | raw AUC | **auc_norm** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 0.900 → 0.750 linear | 0.900 | 0.810 | 0.750 | 0.825 | **0.9167** |
| B: 0.350 → 0.292 linear | 0.350 | 0.315 | 0.292 | 0.321 | **0.9167** |

A is a deployable model at every σ; B never clears R² = 0.35 and is useless for prospective ranking. auc_norm cannot tell them apart — the ratio curves are bit-identical (`np.allclose` True). This is the requested worked pair.

**A2 — it actively rewards a model that was already bad.** A flat, useless model beats a strong, degrading one:

| config | R²(0) | R²(0.6) | R²(1.0) | raw AUC | **auc_norm** | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C: 0.32 → 0.30 | 0.32 | 0.308 | 0.300 | 0.310 | **0.9687** | never usable |
| D: 0.90 → 0.60 | 0.90 | 0.720 | 0.600 | 0.750 | **0.8333** | usable throughout |

C ranks *more robust* than D on the paper's primary metric while being worse than D at every single σ, including σ = 0. The mechanism is not subtle: a model with little signal to lose cannot lose much of it. "Robustness" as measured here is partly just a measure of having started low. This is the direct formal counterexample to `paper.tex:433` ("a high-performing model is not automatically a more robust one") — the converse is the problem: a *low*-performing model is automatically scored as more robust.

**A3 — a small denominator inflates the *variance*, not the level.** Dividing by a small R²(0) does not systematically inflate or deflate the expected value (the ratio curve is invariant), but it amplifies estimation error in the denominator. Since ∂auc_norm/∂R²(0) = −auc_norm/R²(0), sensitivity scales as 1/baseline. Measured, injecting a +0.02 error into the σ=0 point only:

| baseline R²(0) | auc_norm | after +0.02 error on R²(0) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 0.9000 | 0.8469 | **−0.0531** |
| 0.35 | 0.9000 | 0.8541 | −0.0459 |
| 0.50 | 0.9000 | 0.8673 | −0.0327 |
| 0.90 | 0.9000 | 0.8815 | **−0.0185** |

A 0.02 wobble in the clean-label point — well inside replicate noise — moves auc_norm 2.9× more at the 0.3 gate than at 0.9. The gate at 0.3 is therefore admitting exactly the configs where the metric is least stable, which is the opposite of what the code comment at `generate_paper_figures_v2.py:424-428` claims it is doing.

## C) Can R²(σ) go negative, and what the ratio does

**Yes, at both ends, and the guards are incomplete.**

**C1 — negative *baselines* are real and common.** 46 of the 48 rows in `excluded_configs.csv` have R²(0) < 0 (e.g. `dnn_vbll,mol2vec,valprop,-0.1847`; `mlp_vbll,mhggnn,valprop,-0.1899`). These are caught by the 0.3 gate. But the arithmetic if one ever weren't: with a negative denominator the ratio **flips sign**, so a model that gets monotonically worse produces a large *positive* auc_norm. Worked: baseline −0.02 degrading to −25.6 gives **auc_norm = +191.8** — catastrophic failure scored as 192× more robust than a perfect model. The gate is the only thing standing between the pipeline and that number; nothing in `_retention_auc_norm` itself checks the sign.

**C2 — negative R²(σ) at σ > 0 with a positive baseline survives the filters and drags auc_norm below zero.** `filter_catastrophic_iterations` (`:821-873`) drops **whole iterations** where any σ has `r2 < CATASTROPHIC_R2_THRESHOLD = -0.5` (`:436`). R² in the band (−0.5, 0) is *not* filtered. Worked example: baseline 0.35 crossing zero at σ=0.6 and reaching −0.48 gives **auc_norm = −0.0186** — outside the paper's asserted [0,1]. Real evidence in `table_validation_auc_full.csv`: `auc_norm` min = **−0.4033** (OpenADMET-Caco2_Efflux) and **−0.2476** (ChEMBL-hERG-Ki). These are published metric values with the wrong sign relative to the paper's stated range.

**C3 — the far tail is enormous.** `results/paper_figures_v2/filtered_catastrophic_iterations.csv` (245 data rows + header): all 245 r2 values are negative (max −0.5006, by construction of the filter); **min = −1.5486e32** at `mlp,continuous_pdv,valprop,σ=0.4,iter=9` (row 245). Also `dnn,mol2vec,valprop,σ=0.5,iter=0,r2=-2993799936.0`. Note `mlp/continuous_pdv` and `xgboost/pdv` are **not** in `excluded_configs.csv` — they are retained configs whose baseline passed, i.e. a single unfiltered iteration of that magnitude would have driven their auc_norm to ~−1e32. The −0.5 cutoff is doing very heavy, undocumented lifting. Breakdown by σ: **34 of the 245 catastrophic rows are at σ = 0.0** (i.e. the *baseline itself* failed) — including `dnn_bnn_full,mhggnn,threshold,0.0,0,-36.71` and `mlp_vbll,mol2vec,quantile,0.0,2,-1531.51`.

**C4 — a second, undocumented clip.** `VALIDATION_AUC_LOW, VALIDATION_AUC_HIGH = -0.5, 1.1` (`:437`), applied at `:1463-1473`, silently sets out-of-band validation auc_norm to NaN. That accounts for the **7 blank `auc_norm` cells** in `table_validation_auc_full.csv` (465 rows, 7 NaN, 0 NaN in `baseline_r2`) — e.g. `ChEMBL-hERG-Ki,dnn,ecfp4,threshold` and `…,valprop` (rows 4-5). Values of exactly this kind are what auc_norm produces at low baseline; discarding them post hoc removes the evidence of the metric's instability rather than fixing it. Also: 5 configs have auc_norm > 1.0 and survive (max 1.0327, `ChEMBL-hERG-Ki,dnn,mhggnn,outlier`, baseline 0.3159) — "performance improves with noise", again outside the asserted [0,1].

## D) The "decoupled from baseline" claim

Claim under test — `paper.tex:433`: *"Robustness is also decoupled from baseline predictive performance: because AUC$_{norm}$ measures the fraction of baseline R$^2$ retained rather than its absolute level, a high clean-data R$^2$ does not inflate it (Additional file~7)"*; restated at `paper.tex:460` and `paper.tex:573`.

Computed with pandas/scipy on `results/paper_figures_v2/table_validation_auc_full.csv` (458 rows after dropping the 7 NaN auc_norm):

| scope | n | Pearson r | p | Spearman ρ | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **POOLED (3 datasets)** | 458 | **+0.5253** | 7.43e−34 | **+0.5867** | 1.06e−43 |
| ChEMBL-hERG-Ki | 149 | **+0.3037** | 1.67e−04 | +0.3300 | 3.96e−05 |
| OpenADMET-Caco2_Efflux | 153 | +0.1028 | 0.206 | +0.0306 | 0.707 |
| OpenADMET-LogD | 156 | **−0.3060** | 1.02e−04 | −0.4233 | 3.69e−08 |

(These reproduce `deep_baseline_vs_robustness.csv:3-6` exactly — pooled 0.52535/7.432e−34, LogD −0.30600/1.024e−04, Caco2 0.10283/0.2059, hERG 0.30367/1.666e−04. The file is self-consistent; the paper's prose is not consistent with the file.)

Per noise strategy, pooled across datasets — **every one is positive and significant**:

| strategy | n | Pearson r | p | Spearman ρ | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hetero | 77 | +0.5536 | 1.77e−07 | +0.5839 | 2.49e−08 |
| legacy (Gaussian) | 76 | +0.6334 | 8.25e−10 | +0.7564 | 2.76e−15 |
| outlier | 78 | +0.5482 | 2.05e−07 | +0.6556 | 7.34e−11 |
| quantile | 78 | +0.6440 | 2.01e−10 | +0.7826 | 2.64e−17 |
| threshold | 76 | +0.5236 | 1.21e−06 | +0.6070 | 6.13e−09 |
| valprop | 73 | **+0.7048** | 3.41e−12 | +0.7408 | 6.75e−14 |

Dataset × strategy (18 cells) — **the sign is dataset-driven, not strategy-driven**: all 6 LogD cells are negative (−0.419 to −0.633, 6/6 significant at p<0.05); 6/6 hERG-Ki cells are positive (+0.224 to +0.768, 5/6 significant); Caco-2 is mixed and mostly null (hetero −0.5147 p=0.0071 is the only significant one).

**Verdict: the "decoupled" claim does not survive, and the pooled number is a pooling artefact.**

1. **Sign disagreement is real.** Pooled is **+0.53**, but OpenADMET-LogD is **−0.31** — opposite sign, both p ~1e−4. A claim of decoupling cannot be supported by a statistic that changes sign depending on whether you pool.
2. **It is a Simpson's paradox.** Pooling is driven almost entirely by between-dataset differences: dataset means are LogD (baseline 0.7615, auc_norm 0.9414), hERG-Ki (0.5162, 0.7312), Caco-2 (0.4529, 0.5460) — the three dataset means correlate at **r = +0.958** (n=3, p=0.186, not significant at n=3 but obviously the driver). After within-dataset centering the pooled correlation collapses from +0.525 to **+0.1403** (p=2.6e−03) Pearson, and **+0.0566 (p=0.227)** Spearman — i.e. **not significant on ranks**. The pooled +0.53 is measuring "easy datasets are also gentle-degrading datasets", not any within-dataset model property.
3. **But decoupling still fails within datasets.** hERG-Ki is +0.30 (p=1.7e−04) and LogD is −0.31 (p=1.0e−04). Both are significant. So auc_norm is coupled to baseline within two of three datasets — in *opposite directions*. That is worse than a consistent bias: it means the coupling's sign is dataset-dependent and cannot be corrected by a single adjustment.
4. **The paper's cited evidence is stale.** `paper.tex:433` cites Additional file 7 for this claim, and `paper.tex:665` describes Additional file 7 as *"Baseline R$^2$ versus **NDS** scatter (PDV, Gaussian)"* — an NDS figure, the replaced metric, on a single rep × single strategy. It cannot support a claim about auc_norm across the study.
5. **No QM9 support exists.** `deep_baseline_vs_robustness.csv:2` has n=0 for QM9 with the GAP note quoted in B. The decoupling claim at `paper.tex:433`, which sits in the QM9 Results, has **zero numerical backing in this repo**.

**Averaging flags (user rule):** the strategy-pooled table above averages over 3 datasets and the dataset table averages over 6 strategies — each hides the sign flip documented in the 18-cell breakdown, so the 18-cell version is the one that should go in the paper. Separately, every `auc_norm` value in these files already averages over the 10 replicates before the ratio is formed (`generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1818`), which hides the replicate dispersion that A3 shows is 2.9× larger at the 0.3 gate.

**Also flag** — `paper.tex:573` states *"Predictive performance on clean data is not correlated with noise robustness"* as a Conclusions claim, and `paper.tex:460` repeats it. Contradicted by all six strategy correlations above and by hERG-Ki and LogD individually.

## E) When auc_norm and R² at σ = 0.6 disagree — real examples

`table_validation_auc_full.csv` has no per-σ column, so I recomputed both metrics from raw per-σ data at `/Users/apunt/repos/qsar_qm_models/results/validation_full/openadmet_logd/all_results.csv` (columns `sigma,r2,rmse,mae,spearman,model,rep,strategy,dataset`), using the same `trapezoid`/mean-over-iterations recipe as the code. 102 configs, 90 after the ≥0.3 gate (5 models × 4 reps — a **smaller roster than the 156 LogD rows in the figures table**, so these are corroborating, not identical, numbers; on this subset baseline-vs-auc_norm is Pearson −0.0653, p=0.541, vs −0.3060 on the full roster).

Overall agreement is only moderate: **Spearman(auc_norm, R²@0.6) = +0.598, p = 4.86e−10, n = 90**. Two failure modes, both present:

**Mode 1 — auc_norm says robust, R²@0.6 says mediocre.** Mid-baseline tree models with flat curves:

| model | rep | strategy | R²(0) | auc_norm | R²@0.6 | rank by auc_norm | rank by R²@0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| QRF | ECFP4 | outlier | 0.6113 | 0.9925 | 0.6013 | **4** | **71** |
| QRF | ECFP4 | hetero | 0.6113 | 0.9937 | 0.6215 | **3** | **64** |
| QRF | SNS | hetero | 0.6454 | 0.9859 | 0.6384 | 12 | 57 |
| RF | ECFP4 | hetero | 0.6450 | 0.9876 | 0.6437 | 10 | 54 |
| RF | ECFP4 | outlier | 0.6450 | 0.9793 | 0.6296 | 21 | 62 |
| QRF | ECFP4 | quantile | 0.6113 | 0.9494 | 0.5608 | 40 | 77 |

QRF/ECFP4/outlier is 4th-most-robust of 90 and 71st-most-accurate of 90 at realistic noise. This is exactly the A2 mechanism in live data.

**Mode 2 — auc_norm says fragile, R²@0.6 says still the best available.** High-baseline DNNs that lose a lot in relative terms but remain the strongest absolute performers:

| model | rep | strategy | R²(0) | auc_norm | R²@0.6 | rank by auc_norm | rank by R²@0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DNN | MHG-GNN-pretrained | valprop | 0.8339 | 0.8682 | 0.7217 | **69** | **15** |
| DNN | SNS | quantile | 0.8256 | 0.9119 | 0.7514 | 59 | **6** |
| DNN | MHG-GNN-pretrained | quantile | 0.8336 | 0.9302 | 0.7637 | 54 | **5** |
| DNN | MHG-GNN-pretrained | legacy | 0.8150 | 0.9304 | 0.7505 | 53 | **7** |
| DNN | MHG-GNN-pretrained | threshold | 0.8026 | 0.8586 | 0.6808 | 72 | 32 |
| DNN | SNS | threshold | 0.8121 | 0.8062 | 0.6521 | **81** | 48 |

DNN/MHG-GNN/quantile is 54th of 90 on the paper's robustness metric and **5th of 90** on the number that actually decides whether you would deploy it under one unit of real assay error. DNN/SNS/threshold ranks 81st of 90 on auc_norm while still beating 42 configs on absolute R²@0.6.

**The concrete behaviour that makes them disagree:** auc_norm scores the *shape* of the curve and discards its *height*. So it disagrees with R²@0.6 whenever height and shape are anti-correlated — which is precisely the regime present here, because low-baseline models have flatter curves (less to lose) and high-baseline models have steeper ones (more to lose). This is not a fluke of a few configs: the whole Mode-1 block is mid-baseline trees and the whole Mode-2 block is high-baseline DNNs.

The same disagreement is visible in the figures table without per-σ data, using `auc_norm × baseline_r2` (= mean absolute R² over the σ sweep, exactly, by definition of the integral). Spearman between auc_norm and that mean absolute R² is **+0.2952 (p=1.8e−04)** on LogD — i.e. **auc_norm explains under 9% of rank variance in what the model actually delivers across the noise sweep on that dataset** — versus 0.8763 on hERG-Ki and 0.9613 on Caco-2. Concrete cases: `ChEMBL-hERG-Ki,dnn,mhggnn,outlier` has auc_norm 1.0327 (rank 1 of 458) on baseline 0.3159, mean absolute R² 0.3262 — top-ranked robustness, near-useless model. Conversely `OpenADMET-LogD,qrf,sns,threshold` has auc_norm 0.7352 (bottom of LogD) with mean absolute R² 0.5733, better than almost every hERG or Caco-2 config.

**The user's instinct is confirmed by the data.** R² at σ=0.6 catches exactly the two classes auc_norm misses: (i) flat-because-empty models that auc_norm promotes, (ii) high-ceiling models that auc_norm demotes despite still being the best choice under realistic noise. Per the stored context, σ=0.6 ≈ one unit of real experimental error (hERG pKi SD 0.54, logD 0.27–0.62, Caco-2 ~0.43), so it is the decision-relevant operating point, not an arbitrary one.

**Data needed to extend this to QM9 and to hERG-Ki:** per-σ, per-iteration R² for those datasets is not in this repo (only `openadmet_logd/all_results.csv` and the 24-file `openadmet_caco2/` directory exist under `results/validation_full/`; `openadmet_caco2/` has no `all_results.csv`, and there is no hERG directory). Required: the server dump named in `deep_baseline_vs_robustness.csv:2` (`qm9_auc_with_baseline.csv`, valprop-guarded, or the raw `anova_*.csv`), plus per-σ hERG-Ki and Caco-2 results.

## F) Recommended metric

**Put a two-number pair in the paper: absolute R² at σ = 0.6 as the primary, with auc_norm retained as a shape descriptor.** Reasoning against each candidate:

1. **Absolute R² at a fixed realistic σ (recommended primary).** Directly answers the deployment question; no denominator, so no small-baseline instability (A3), no sign flip (C1), no [0,1] violation (C4). σ = 0.6 is defensible from published assay-error SDs rather than chosen for convenience. It is already the discriminator that exposes both failure modes in E. Weakness: it is one point on a curve and ignores everything else — which is why it should not travel alone. Note the paper already uses R²@σ=0.3 as its performance outcome in the ANOVA (`generate_paper_figures_v2.py:2146`, `:2259`; described at `paper.tex:387`), so a fixed-σ absolute R² is an established quantity in this pipeline, not a new construct.

2. **Unnormalised AUC (mean absolute R² across the sweep) — recommended companion, better than the current metric.** `∫R²(σ)dσ / σ_max`. Keeps the whole-curve information auc_norm was meant to capture, but on the absolute scale, so it cannot reward a model for having nothing to lose. Already computable from existing outputs as `auc_norm × baseline_r2` (exact identity), so it costs one column, no re-run. In the A1 pair it separates A (0.825) from B (0.321) that auc_norm called identical, and in A2 it correctly ranks D (0.750) above C (0.310). Downside: dominated by baseline, so it is a performance metric that is noise-aware rather than a robustness metric — it should be reported *alongside* auc_norm, not instead of it.

3. **σ at which R² falls below a usable threshold ("noise tolerance", e.g. σ where R² first drops under 0.5).** Attractive interpretability, but **reject as primary**: it is a censored quantity. Configs that never cross the threshold within σ ≤ 1.0 are right-censored (all 6 LogD `hetero` configs in the top-12 sit above 0.69 at σ=1.0), and configs starting below the threshold are undefined at σ=0 — with the 0.3 gate, that is a large fraction of the Caco-2 roster (mean baseline 0.4529). Needs survival-analysis handling; not worth it here.

4. **Retention ratio at a single σ (R²(0.6)/R²(0)) — reject.** It has every pathology of auc_norm (same denominator, same sign flip, same variance amplification) with strictly less information, since it uses one point instead of eleven. Strictly dominated.

**Concrete recommendation.** Report, disaggregated by strategy × σ × model × rep with no cross-strategy averaging:
- **R²(σ=0.6)** as the headline robustness-relevant number, justified from assay-error magnitudes;
- **auc_norm** kept, but explicitly relabelled as a *retention-shape* statistic, not a robustness ranking, with the [0,1] claim at `paper.tex:247` corrected to the observed range (min −0.4033, max 1.0327 in `table_validation_auc_full.csv`);
- the **rank-disagreement between the two** reported as a finding in its own right — Spearman 0.598 on the LogD raw data, with the QRF/ECFP4 (rank 4 vs 71) and DNN/MHG-GNN (rank 54 vs 5) cases named. That disagreement is the interesting result, and it is currently invisible because only auc_norm is published.
