## E) Settling the PDV contradiction

- `paper.tex:462` — "PDV stood out as having particularly strong robustness to noise. This suggests that PDVs are preferable when dealing with noisy data."
- `paper.tex:493` — "Across all six noise strategies, PDV configurations produced the shallowest mean NDSs across representations."
- `paper.tex:567` — "PDVs produce the strongest predictive performance on clean data, but are the least noise-resistant."

**On QM9 — the data the main figures and the ANOVA come from — line 567 is factually wrong.** `deep_qm9_rep_robustness_by_strategy.csv` ranks continuous_pdv 2nd, 3rd, 3rd, 3rd, 4th and 4th of 8–9 representations across the six strategies, and it beats ecfp4, smiles, mhggnn, sns and randomized_smiles in **all six**. It is beaten only by binary `pdv` (all six) and by mol2vec (four of six). PDV is **never last in any strategy**. "Least noise-resistant" is contradicted by every one of the six QM9 numbers.

**Line 567's other half is also unsupported.** There is no QM9 clean-data baseline in the v2 outputs at all — `results/paper_figures_v2/deep_baseline_vs_robustness.csv` row 0 is an explicit gap ("QM9 baseline_r2 ... NEEDS server dump qm9_auc_with_baseline.csv"), n = 0. On validation (`table_validation_auc_full.csv`, `baseline_r2` by rep), continuous_pdv is **last of 4** on hERG-Ki (0.4750 vs sns 0.5346) and **last of 4** on Caco2-Efflux (0.4164 vs ecfp4 0.4852), and 3rd of 4 on LogD (0.7587 vs mhggnn 0.7783). "PDVs produce the strongest predictive performance on clean data" is **contradicted on all three external datasets** and has NO SOURCE FOUND for QM9.

**Verdict: delete `paper.tex:567`'s claim entirely.** Both of its assertions fail against local data.

**Lines 462 and 493 are directionally right but need repair before use:**
- 462's "particularly strong robustness" is true in the mean-rank sense (2nd–4th of 8–9, top-half in all six strategies), but "PDVs are preferable when dealing with noisy data" overstates it — the mean gap between PDV and ECFP4 under Gaussian is 0.8298 vs 0.8254, i.e. 0.0044, which is smaller than the within-rep model spread (0.0185 std) by a factor of 4. The honest statement is "PDV is among the more robust representations, but the difference between representations is small compared with the difference between models."
- 493's "shallowest mean NDSs" is wrong twice: NDS is the retired metric, and on auc_norm PDV is not the top rep in any strategy (binary `pdv` is). Rewrite in auc_norm and drop "shallowest".

**Where line 567 has a grain of truth, and it must be stated honestly rather than deleted silently:** on the external Caco2-Efflux dataset, continuous_pdv **is** the least robust of the four reps under Gaussian (0.2752 vs mhggnn 0.4957), quantile (0.3345, last) and threshold (0.5760, last) — `table_validation_auc_full.csv`. Restricting to the common 5 models it stays last under Gaussian, quantile and threshold. So PDV's QM9 advantage does **not** transfer to Caco2. That is a real, reportable result and it is a much stronger paper sentence than either of the two current contradictory ones.

## F) Recommendation

**Representation for the main figures: keep continuous_pdv, but only if the two things below are shown alongside it.** The case for keeping it: it has the full 11-model roster (only ecfp4, binary pdv and smiles also do), and it is genuinely mid-to-upper in robustness in all six strategies, so it is not a cherry-pick of the winner. The case against: it has the 2nd-narrowest model spread of any rep in all six strategies and the 3rd-lowest model-effect eta² (59.08% Gaussian vs 86.52% on smiles), so it is the substrate that most understates the paper's own headline effect.

**Strategy for the main figures: keep Gaussian (`legacy`) as the single-strategy panel.** Model rank under Gaussian on PDV matches the six-strategy mean rank for 9 of 11 models (only dnn and mlp_vbll swap by one place), and Gaussian–Hetero Spearman is 1.000, Gaussian–ValProp 0.973, Gaussian–Threshold 0.964, Gaussian–Quantile 0.955. It is a faithful proxy for rank. It is **not** a faithful proxy for level.

**What must be shown alongside, or the choice is hiding something:**

1. **The rep × model auc_norm grid for the Gaussian panel, all 9 reps.** Otherwise "MLPs are least robust" (PDV rank 11) travels as a general claim while mol2vec puts mlp at rank 4. Table A above is the artefact.

2. **All six strategies on the same axis for PDV, showing level not just rank.** PDV mean auc_norm runs 0.9446 (outlier) to 0.6032 (threshold). Fig 1b already does this for the heatmap; the degradation-curve panel (Fig 1a) and `fig_nn_family_comparison` do not, and their caption "Heteroscedastic results (not shown) are qualitatively consistent" is unverifiable from a figure that is not there.

3. **SVM's outlier-noise result must appear somewhere prominent.** It is the one genuine rank inversion: 5th under Gaussian (0.8388), 1st under outlier (0.9556). A Gaussian-only main figure asserts a model order in which SVM never leads.

4. **A per-strategy model-spread strip** (range 0.0233 outlier → 0.1288 threshold on PDV), so a reader knows which panel discriminates architectures and which does not. This is already argued at `paper.tex:460` and the numbers check out; it needs to be visible, not only stated.

5. **The missing-cell map (Table in D) as a supplementary figure, with the roster size printed on every rep column.** Three specific corrections are mandatory: (a) the exclusion count at `paper.tex:464` is 48, not 24; (b) the GP claim at `paper.tex:427` has no supporting continuous_pdv data; (c) the validation PDV panels are missing dnn and svm, and either the figure states that on its face or the validation comparison is restricted to the common 5 models.

6. **The Kendall W sentence at `paper.tex:433` must say which data it is computed on.** As it stands it reads as a PDV statistic inside a PDV paragraph and is a 9-rep average. Report **W = 0.9374 on PDV** if the paragraph is about PDV, or report the per-rep range **0.6885 (MHG-GNN) to 0.9722 (SNS)** if the claim is meant to be general — the averaged 0.9121 states neither and hides that concordance collapses on learned embeddings.
