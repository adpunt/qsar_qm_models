**Discrepancy 1 — the threshold cut is applied to RAW targets, not normalised ones.** `generate_value_based_noise_map` calls `read_all_target_values(config)` (`rust/src/main.rs:167`), which reads `smiles_data.target_value` straight from `train_{}.mmap` (`rust/src/main.rs:173-190`). Standardisation happens *after* noise addition, in `write_data`: `property_value = (property_value - mean) / std_dev` (`rust/src/main.rs:759-761`), using mean/SD of the *already-noisy* training labels (`rust/src/main.rs:959-978`). So `paper.tex:354` "(on normalized data)" is factually wrong about the implementation. (Outlier is unaffected — a z-score is scale-invariant. Quantile is unaffected — percentiles are order-statistics. Valprop and hetero ARE affected: `0.1|y|` and `0.05|y|` are evaluated on eV-scale numbers ≈6.9, not on z-scores ≈0.)

**Discrepancy 2 — `scripts/noise_strategy_params.json` is NEVER passed to the Rust binary.** The Popen argv is `scripts/process_and_train.py:1628-1641`:
```python

    [
        rust_executable_path,
        '--seed', str(iteration_seed),
        '--model', "rf",
        '--sigma', str(s),
        '--noise_distribution', args.distribution,
        '--noise_strategy', args.noise_strategy,
    ],
```
There is no `--strategy_params`. The argument is *declared* at `scripts/process_and_train.py:260` (`default="noise_strategy_params.json"`) and `args.strategy_params` appears nowhere else in the repo (`grep -n "strategy_params" scripts/*.py` returns only line 260). Rust therefore takes the `else` branch at `rust/src/main.rs:1155-1157`, `strategy_params` = empty JSON object, and every `unwrap_or(...)` default at `rust/src/main.rs:1180-1230` applies. Consequences:
- The JSON's `"proportionality_factor": 0.05` was never read; the effective value is the Rust default `0.1` (`rust/src/main.rs:1181`), which is what the paper states. The paper is right only by accident.
- The JSON's `"base_sigma": 0.1` was never read; effective `base_sigma = σ` (`rust/src/main.rs:1180`). **Latent landmine:** if anyone ever passes that JSON, valprop stops scaling with σ entirely (fixed base 0.1) and every valprop curve becomes flat. 
- All other JSON values happen to equal the Rust defaults, so no other numeric change.

**Discrepancy 3 — the methods figure does not use QM9.** `paper.tex:359` captions Fig. `fig_methods_noise_strategies` as "Effect of each noise injection strategy on the **QM9 HOMO–LUMO gap** label distribution … at σ=0.5". It also re-implements two strategies *differently from Rust*:
- threshold: `multipliers = np.where(y > median, 2.0, 0.1)` — `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:2561-2562`. A **median** split (50/50), not `|y|>1`, and not the Rust `y>=1.0`. Three different definitions in three places.
- valprop: `noise = np.random.normal(0,1,n) * (sigma + 0.1*np.abs(y))` — `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:2557`. **Additive** `σ + 0.1|y|`; Rust is **multiplicative** `σ(1+0.1|y|)` (`rust/src/main.rs:313`). Differ whenever σ≠1.

The per-panel `RMSE=` annotations (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:2626-2628`) are therefore doses of the *synthetic* mixture under *reimplemented* strategies, not of the experiment.

**Discrepancy 4 (one line, collides with everything above).** `paper.tex:313` states "Validation and test data remain free of noise." But — critically — **normalisation is applied after noise injection and using the noisy SD**, so no strategy ever sees a unit-variance target.

## B) Analytic RMS injected noise at σ=1

Define the dose factor `D = sqrt( (1/N) Σ_i (s_i/σ)² )`. Because every `s_i` is exactly linear in σ (multiplier×σ for outlier/quantile/threshold; `σ(1+0.1|y|)` for valprop; `σ√(0.1+0.05|y|)` for hetero), **D is a constant per strategy and RMS dose = D·σ exactly.**

### B1. The counterfactual the paper implies (targets ~ N(0,1))

| Strategy | Arithmetic | RMS at σ=1 | Fraction elevated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gaussian | 1 | **1.0000** | n/a |
| Outlier | P(\|z\|>2)=2Φ(−2)=0.045500; √(0.045500·9 + 0.954500·0.01) = √0.419045 | **0.6473** | 4.55% |
| Quantile | 20% by construction; √(0.2·4 + 0.8·0.01) = √0.808 | **0.8989** | 20.00% |
| Threshold | P(\|y\|>1)=2Φ(−1)=0.317311; √(0.317311·4 + 0.682689·0.01) = √1.276076 | **1.1296** | 31.73% |
| Valprop | E[(1+0.1\|y\|)²] = 1 + 0.2·E\|y\| + 0.01·E[y²], E\|y\|=√(2/π)=0.797885 → 1.169577; √ | **1.0815** | n/a (continuous) |
| Hetero | E[0.1+0.05\|y\|] = 0.1 + 0.05·0.797885 = 0.139894; √ | **0.3740** | n/a (continuous) |

Even in this idealised world the doses span **3.02×** in RMS (9.1× in variance), from hetero 0.374 to threshold 1.130.

## C) The threshold cut is degenerate on QM9

- **For a standard normal**, `|y|>1` captures 2Φ(−1) = **31.73%** above, **68.27%** within → RMS 1.1296 (B1).
- **For QM9 as actually run**, the cut `value >= 1.0` (`rust/src/main.rs:367`) is compared against the **raw eV gap**, whose minimum is **0.6694 eV**. Fraction with |y| ≤ 1.0 eV = **7.64 × 10⁻⁶ — one molecule out of 130,832.** Fraction receiving the 2σ multiplier = **99.9992%**.

**Therefore threshold noise on QM9 is not a threshold strategy at all. It is homoscedastic Gaussian noise at exactly double dose.** The coefficient of variation of its per-molecule scale is **0.0026** — the second-most-uniform of the six, and effectively identical to Gaussian's 0.000. Its `mean s_i` = 2.0000 and `RMS` = 2.0000 to four decimals.

Note the near-miss the other way: had the pipeline stayed in Hartree (raw CSV gap: mean 0.2511, max 0.6221, `data/QM9/raw/gdb9.sdf.csv`), **100% of molecules would have fallen below the cut** and threshold would have been the *weakest* strategy at 0.1σ. The strategy's entire character is an accident of the eV/Hartree unit choice inherited from PyG, not a design decision.

Evidence in the repo about the QM9 gap distribution: `data/QM9/raw/gdb9.sdf.csv` (column `gap`, Hartree) and `data/QM9/raw/uncharacterized.txt`; unit conversion at `torch_geometric/datasets/qm9.py:17-23`. No file in `results/` records the realised dose per strategy — **NO SOURCE FOUND** for any in-repo record of injected-noise magnitude on QM9.

## D) VERDICT: the "architectural discrimination" finding is a dose artifact

Paper claims under test:
- `paper.tex:460`: "The spread of AUC_norm across models differed by noise strategy, with **outlier noise having a spread of 0.02 and threshold noise having 0.13**, presumably exposing architectural differences that are masked by other types of noise."
- `paper.tex:573`: "Threshold and value-proportional noise produce the widest spread of **NDS** across models; outlier noise barely separates them." — ⚠️ **stale metric: NDS was replaced by auc_norm** (`scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1791-1801`, `:1806`). `paper.tex` still says NDS in 19 places (lines 387, 462, 464, 470, 475, 493, 495, 551, 556, 571, 573, 598, 660, 661, 662, 664, 665, 666, 669).

I reproduced the spreads from `results/paper_figures_v2/table2_auc_by_strategy_pdv.csv` (11 models × 6 strategies, PDV) and joined them to the doses from B2:

| Strategy | D (eV) | noise var. share | mean AUC_norm | min | max | **range (spread)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outlier | 0.490 | 0.1273 | 0.9446 | 0.9322 | 0.9556 | **0.0233** |
| Hetero | 0.665 | 0.2117 | 0.9110 | 0.8916 | 0.9217 | **0.0301** |
| Quantile | 0.900 | 0.3296 | 0.8505 | 0.8160 | 0.8729 | **0.0569** |
| Gaussian | 1.000 | 0.3775 | 0.8298 | 0.7892 | 0.8508 | **0.0616** |
| Valprop | 1.691 | 0.6341 | 0.6653 | 0.5902 | 0.7007 | **0.1105** |
| Threshold | 2.000 | 0.7081 | 0.6032 | 0.5160 | 0.6448 | **0.1288** |

(Outlier 0.0233 ≈ the paper's "0.02"; threshold 0.1288 ≈ the paper's "0.13". The paper's two numbers reproduce. Both are **spreads across models within one representation (PDV)**; each cell is itself a mean over 10 replicates via `avg_group = group.groupby('sigma')['r2'].mean()` at `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1820`, and auc_norm integrates over the whole σ grid — so the "spread" hides both replicate variance and any σ-dependence of the model ordering.)

Correlations across the six strategies:

- **Spearman ρ(dose D, mean AUC_norm) = −1.0000** (perfect, all six)
- **Spearman ρ(dose D, cross-model spread) = +1.0000** (perfect, all six)
- Pearson r(D, mean AUC_norm) = −0.9996; Pearson r(D, spread) = +0.9965
- Pearson r(D, mean AUC_norm) using noise-variance share instead of D = −0.9954

**Verdict: not a finding about noise structure. It is a dose ranking, with zero residual signal.** The evidence:

1. The rank order of "severity" is the rank order of injected dose, exactly, with no inversions.
2. The rank order of "architectural discrimination" is the same rank order, exactly, with no inversions.
3. **The decisive test:** threshold — the strategy singled out as revealing architecture — has a per-molecule scale CV of **0.0026** (B2/C). It has essentially no structure: 99.9992% of molecules get the identical multiplier. It is Gaussian noise at 2×. Meanwhile outlier — the strategy said to "barely separate" models — has the *most* structured noise of all six (CV = 2.629, a 30× per-molecule range) and is the one that separates models *least*. **The relationship between structural heterogeneity and model discrimination is inverted from the paper's story, and is fully explained once dose is accounted for.**
4. Mechanically, spread widens with dose because auc_norm is a retention fraction bounded near 1 at low dose: at outlier's dose all eleven models retain 93–96% and are compressed against the ceiling; at threshold's dose retention falls to 52–64% and models fan out. This is a floor/ceiling effect on the metric, not architecture becoming visible.
5. The root cause is that `auc_norm` integrates over the **nominal** σ (`_retention_auc_norm(sig, r2, base)`, `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:1791-1801`; `srange = sig.max()-sig.min()`), and σ is a different physical quantity in each strategy. Comparing strategies at equal σ is comparing them at unequal dose.

Corollary: the ANOVA claim at `paper.tex:460` — "outlier and heteroscedastic noise leave most of the AUC_norm variance in the residual term, precisely where the spread is narrowest" — is the same artifact. Those two strategies inject the least noise (D = 0.490, 0.665), so between-model signal is smallest relative to replicate variance, so the residual share is largest. It is a signal-to-noise statement about the experiment, not about the models.

The one claim in this region that **survives** dose-matching concerns is the concordance: `paper.tex:573` Kendall's W = 0.92 across strategies. Perfectly consistent with the dose story — one underlying model ordering, rescaled six times. (I did not verify 0.92; **NO SOURCE FOUND** for W in `results/paper_figures_v2/`.)

## E) A fair, dose-matched comparison

**Held constant:** the realised RMS injected noise, not σ. Define, per strategy, on the training targets of the run:

```
D_strategy = sqrt( mean_i ( s_i / σ )² )      # constant, computed once from y_train
δ         = D_strategy · σ                    # RMS injected noise, eV
δ_norm    = δ / SD(y_clean_train)             # RMS noise in clean-target SD units
```
For QM9 with SD(y) = 1.2842 eV: D = {gaussian 1.0000, threshold 2.0000, valprop 1.6907, quantile 0.9004, hetero 0.6655, outlier 0.4904}.

**What changes:** replace the x-axis σ with δ_norm everywhere, and recompute auc_norm as a trapezoid over a **common δ_norm grid** rather than over σ. Because D is an exact constant per strategy (B), this is a pure linear rescaling of each strategy's x-axis — no re-derivation of the metric is needed.

**The common window.** With the existing grid σ ∈ {0.0, 0.1, …, 1.0} (`scripts/generate_anova_slurm_scripts.py:49`), the highest dose every strategy can reach is set by outlier at σ=1: δ_max = 0.4904 eV, i.e. **δ_norm ∈ [0, 0.3819]**. The σ needed to hit that ceiling: outlier 1.000, hetero 0.737, quantile 0.545, gaussian 0.490, valprop 0.290, threshold 0.245.

**Figure spec — a 3-panel companion to Fig. 1:**
- **(a)** Retention curves R²(σ)/R²(0) plotted against δ_norm, one line per strategy, faceted by model, all 6 strategies shown together (no averaging across strategies). Under the dose hypothesis the six lines collapse onto one curve per model.
- **(b)** Dose-matched auc_norm: for each (model, rep, strategy), the trapezoid of retention over the *common* δ_norm ∈ [0, 0.3819], plotted as the same heatmap as Fig. 1b. Under the dose hypothesis every row becomes flat across strategies.
- **(c)** The diagnostic scatter that makes the confound explicit: x = D_strategy (or noise-variance share), y = cross-model spread of auc_norm, six points, with the Spearman ρ = 1.00 annotated — plus a second series showing the dose-matched spread, which should show no trend.

**What it would show.** Three distinguishable outcomes, and the figure discriminates them: (i) curves collapse and panel-b rows go flat → the strategy effect was entirely dose, and `paper.tex:460` and `:573` must be retracted and replaced with the concordance result; (ii) curves collapse but residual spread differences survive → a genuine, small structure effect, now quantifiable; (iii) outlier/quantile depart from the collapsed bundle (their CVs are 2.63 and 1.58, so they are the only two with real structural heterogeneity) → the real finding is "concentrated noise is *easier* to absorb than diffuse noise at equal dose", the opposite of the current claim.

**Also required alongside it:** report D and the per-molecule CV of s_i for all six strategies as a Methods table, replacing `tab:regression_noise`'s σ-only column with an explicit "RMS dose at σ=1" column; and regenerate `fig_methods_noise_strategies` from the actual QM9 training targets through the actual Rust definitions rather than the synthetic mixture at `scripts/generate_paper_figures_v2.py:2541-2562`.
