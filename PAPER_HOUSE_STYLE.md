# Flow of the argument

## What this is

Forty-three rules for the paper's Results and discussion and Conclusions, each one traceable to a named paper and a quote, each one written as something you can check against a draft in a few minutes. The rules come from nineteen reference papers. Six of them are in the target journal: Kolmar and Grulke 2021, Dablander 2023, Mervin and Trapotsi 2021, Venkatraman 2021, Lapins 2018, Wang 2023. Nine are Nature Machine Intelligence. Four are Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling or preprints.

Where the target-journal papers and the Nature Machine Intelligence papers disagree, the target journal wins, and the rule says so.

## The six places they disagree, and the ruling

**One.** How many numbers go in the running text. Venkatraman carries no head-to-head number in its results at all. Dablander gives levels only, "around 0.7", "tends to exceed 0.9". Kolmar's only prose numbers are two anchors to assay error. Against that, ImageMol prints "AUC = 0.837 ... AUC = 0.704" and ChatNT prints "average MCC of 0.77 versus 0.71". **Target journal wins: coarse levels and anchors in the text, per-cell values in the tables.** This is also what you asked for.

**Two.** Whether there is a standalone Discussion. Heid, Hirschfeld and Scalia all keep one. Dablander, Mervin and Landrum use "Results and discussion" then a short closing section; Kolmar uses "Results" then "Discussion and conclusions". **Target journal wins: one section, then Conclusions, nothing between them.**

**Three.** Where caveats live. ImageMol collects them into a paragraph opening "We acknowledge several limitations." None of the target-journal papers has a limitations paragraph anywhere; every caveat is welded to the sentence it limits. **Target journal wins.**

**Four.** How often the headline is restated. ImageMol states it five times, GEM four. Dablander states each finding once. **Target journal wins: once, in its own block.**

**Five.** Whether the reader is told what to do. Five of the nine Nature Machine Intelligence papers give no advice at all. All six target-journal papers end a block on a recommendation or a decision rule. **Target journal wins: every block ends on one.**

**Six.** Whether headings state the finding. Cui and the SPECTRA paper use claim-shaped headings. Kolmar, Dablander, Venkatraman, Mervin and Lapins name the measured quantity. **Target journal wins: headings name the quantity or the question.**

Two more rules hold in the target journal and have no counterpart elsewhere, because no Nature Machine Intelligence paper in the set injects noise and none reports an uncertainty result of its own. Those are the rule about anchoring noise levels to published assay error, and the rule about opening the uncertainty half with why precision is a different question from accuracy.

## The rules

### Naming and quantities

1. No coined name for a measured quantity. Everything is either a metric named in the literature or a project name defined once in Methods. Anything else is written out.
2. A derived quantity is named by what it is made of. Swing, cost, penalty, budget, headroom, price and tax are banned as names for numbers.
3. If a derived quantity cannot be avoided, it gets four sentences before its first value: what it is made of, what a large value means, what a small value means, when it misbehaves. AUC_norm needs all four.
4. A term the study coins is defined once and used unchanged. AUC_norm is never described instead of named. PDV is never called a descriptor vector.

### Numbers

5. Print the raw pair before the derived number.
6. Every count is N out of M named things.
7. Every number carries its unit in the same sentence.
8. The dataset's own metric first, the derived number second.
9. Anchor the noise levels to published assay error in log units, at the first noise result. The levels are a fraction of the clean training label spread, so the sentence has to convert.
10. Few numbers in the running text.
11. No number averages over datasets or over representations. A cross-dataset summary is a count of wins or a rank test.
12. QM9 and the three assay datasets never share a flat sentence about the study.

### Figures and tables

13. Say what one row is, what each axis holds, and in what unit, before the first value is read off it.
14. Every caption says what the spread is measured over and what any marking means.

### Sentences

15. One fact per sentence. The consequence gets its own sentence. Nothing over thirty words.
16. Mechanism is hedged. Measurement is not.
17. A caveat is welded to the claim. No limitations paragraph inside the results.
18. Ask the reader's question in the text before answering it.
19. Where two conditions differ in one thing, say which thing.

### Blocks

20. Headings name the measured quantity or the question, never the finding, never a piece of the work.
21. A block opens by pointing at where its numbers live or by stating its finding.
22. Blocks are joined by a sentence saying what the previous block does not yet establish.
23. Where QM9 and an assay result are read against each other, say so and name the earlier block.
24. Every block ends on one plain-English sentence a reader can act on or believe differently.
25. Every recommendation carries its condition and its cost in the same sentence.
26. State each finding once.
27. The surprising finding is stated where it is measured, in the first two sentences of that block, and labelled surprising.
28. One representative figure in the text, the rest to the Additional file, signalled once.
29. A significance test is a clause or a footnote, never its own block.

### Honesty

30. A cell whose clean accuracy is near zero is declared unreadable, not scored for robustness.
31. Every exclusion is named where it happens, with its reason and where the full version lives.
32. A control is introduced by the objection it answers.
33. Null results and results that go against the finding get the same voice and the same place.
34. Behaviour with no explanation is written as having no explanation.
35. A weak result is attributed in the same sentence as the number, with the count that makes it weak.
36. Where nothing wins everywhere, say so and refuse to rank.
37. Prior work is cited beside the claim it bears on, in that paper's own numbers.

### Architecture

38. The split type, the replicate count, the scoring rule and the level grid are stated once, early.
39. Results and discussion is one section, then Conclusions.
40. Conclusions restates in words with no new measurement.
41. Conclusions walks the same questions in the same order and ends on what the study could not settle.
42. The uncertainty half opens with why precision is a different question from accuracy, and what the estimates depend on.
43. Where an uncertainty statistic is separated into two parts, say for each row whether each part varies per molecule or is one number per fit.

## How to run this on a draft

Six passes, each looking for one thing.

Pass one, names. Every noun phrase that names a number. If it is not in Methods, it fails.

Pass two, numbers. Every number. Unit attached, denominator attached, raw pair present, no average across datasets or representations.

Pass three, first and last sentences. Read only the first and last sentence of every subsection. The first states the finding or points at the numbers. The last is plain English and tells the reader something to do.

Pass four, the joins. Read the boundaries between subsections. Each one says what the previous block does not establish.

Pass five, what is missing. For each block, find the sentence naming the case where the pattern does not hold, and the sentence naming what was excluded. If neither is there, the block is not finished.

Pass six, Conclusions. Every number in it appears earlier. Every paragraph matches a subsection, in order.

# House style and wording

# House style for the noise-robustness paper, checked against nineteen reference papers

## What this is

Thirty-five rules, each with a way to check a draft against it. Every rule traces to named papers and quoted sentences. Where the three Journal of Cheminformatics papers disagree with the Nature Machine Intelligence ones, the target journal wins and the rule says so.

Three things were checked against the repository rather than against the papers: the journal's own section rule, the settled condition names, and the coined terms already sitting in the revision guide. Everything else comes from the reference-paper survey.

## Five things in the revision guide fail these rules today

These are in `PAPER_REVISION_GUIDE_FINAL.md`, which is where the paper's replacement text lives. The first three are coined names for measured quantities. The literature has no equivalent: across nineteen papers there are four coinages and every one is a symbol or an acronym defined by its construction.

| where | what it says | what it is | what to write instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| line 6438, 6454, 6469 | "clean R² swing" | best minus worst clean R² a model reaches across the six representations | say that, in a sentence |
| line 6438, 6454, 6470 | "noise-cost R² swing" | best minus worst amount of R² the noise destroys, across the same six | say that, in a sentence |
| line 6493, and line 4625 inside proposed paper text | "the shared-offset condition costs" | R² lost across the noise range under `grouped_shifted`, over and above plain Gaussian | name the condition `grouped_shifted` and say what was subtracted from what |

Your guess about the third one was close. It is the R² the noise destroys under `grouped_shifted` minus the R² it destroys under plain Gaussian, at the same doses, medianed over the models that ran.

`grouped_shifted` is not a free choice of words. `condition_names.json` says it is the only place the name is written down, both injectors are checked against it, and results rows from the two pipelines are joined on that string. A second name for the same condition is a naming rule that cannot be joined.

## The rules, grouped

### Naming

1. No English noun is the name of a measured quantity. Only standard metrics, or a symbol whose construction is in the sentence that first uses it.
2. Replace the five coined terms above.
3. Conditions are named by the string in `condition_names.json`.
4. AUC_norm and PDV, always, name before description.
5. One sentence, in the text and in the first caption, saying AUC_norm is not an ROC area.

### Numbers

6. Only three kinds of number in prose: a threshold that sorts results into a named band, a count of datasets or conditions, an absolute anchor in the dataset's own units. No grid cell.
7. Under about 12% of Results sentences carry a decimal. Kolmar runs at 3.9%, Dablander at 12%, and the two papers at 25% are the least readable in the survey.
8. Every number carries its unit or denominator in the same sentence, before the number. Counts are "N out of M named things".
9. A derived number never stands alone. Print the raw pair, in the dataset's own metric, in the same sentence. If the pair cannot be printed, the derived number stays out.

This is the rule set that serves your brief directly. The tables carry the grid; the prose carries directions, bands and counts.

### Comparisons

10. Both sides named, the metric named, the scope named, in one sentence.
11. Head-to-head claims are win counts with the denominator printed. Never an average across representations or datasets. The literature agrees with your own no-averaging rule: not one of the nineteen papers reports a mean across representations as a headline.
12. Say which direction is good, in a parenthesis, at the first comparison of each metric.

### Shape

13. Measurement in one sentence, consequence in the next. Drop the "This indicates that" connector, keep the two-sentence shape.
14. Every Results paragraph ends on a consequence sentence, not on a number and not on a figure pointer. Read the last sentence alone; if it still holds a measurement, the paragraph is unfinished.
15. One paragraph, one object. 100 to 200 words, three to seven sentences.
16. Median sentence 20 to 26 words, nothing over about 55 unless it is a roster of names.

### Voice

17. Hedge the mechanism, never the measurement, and name the alternative the hedge stands in for.
18. Hedge at 8 to 19 per thousand words of Results. Below 8 the draft reads like the wrong journal. The three target-journal papers run 7.8 to 19.3; the Nature ones run 1.7 to 5.7.
19. Keep "we" out of results sentences, at 2 to 6 per thousand words. The subject of a finding is the measured thing.
20. Shows, indicates, observed, reveals, found. Not demonstrate, not achieve, never prove. Not state-of-the-art, not novel.
21. No bare trailing "(Fig. 3a)". The three target-journal papers have zero; the Nature ones have four to nineteen.

### What must be stated

22. A failed, null or degenerate result leads its own sentence, in its own block, with no rescue clause. This covers `gp_collapsed`, the question that is undefined on held-out molecules under a scaffold split, and every representation that loses.
23. Limitations sit next to the claim they bound. No Limitations heading, no "we acknowledge". An unexplained result may stand unexplained.
24. Say what AUC_norm cannot show, in the block that introduces it. Kolmar criticises his own slope ratio in the same section that uses it.
25. Anchor the noise against a published assay standard deviation in log units, once, in the Results. The fraction-of-spread scale stays; the log-unit equivalent is printed once beside it.
26. Check one predicted uncertainty against a number measured outside the model.

### Where things live

27. Captions answer five things, in the same words every time: what one mark is, both axes with units, what the error bar spans, how many replicates, which region is good. The prose never describes panel layout.
28. Relegation is one parenthesis inside the finding sentence. A condition that did not run gets a prose sentence where a reader would ask.
29. Each finding once, in its own block. Later blocks invert or qualify; they do not repeat.
30. Headings name the measure or the question. No Q1, no R3. The R1 to R7 and Q1, Q2 labels in the planning document must not reach the paper.
31. Decide once about significance tests and hold to it.
32. At most one recommendation sentence per block, naming a specific model or pairing.
33. Prior work engaged by name, inline, with what it did not cover. The non-Gaussian conditions are introduced against Kolmar's own stated restriction, in his words.
34. Introduction, Methods, Results and discussion, Conclusions. No standalone Discussion.

## Four calls that are yours

Each side is what a target-journal paper actually does, so both are defensible.

**Significance tests.** Run none, and carry uncertainty in the replicate structure plus an error bar defined in every caption: Kolmar, Dablander and Heid all do this, with zero p-values across 8,772, 6,088 and 11,199 words. Or run them and print the test, the correction and the threshold before the first result, including for the nulls: Svensson and Boulougouri do this. Cost of the second: a test attached to every comparative claim, and "significant" can then never be used loosely.

**Decimals in the Results prose.** 4% of sentences, which is Kolmar, the closest published analogue. Or 12%, which is Dablander. Both are the target journal.

**Tables or figures.** Kolmar carries the grid in tables, 16 table references against 7 figure references, and shows two exemplar curves. Dablander carries everything in figures, 12 figure references and no results table at all. With 13 to 19 models by 6 representations by 7 conditions by 4 datasets, this decides how the grid is read.

**Recommendations in the Results.** Dablander gives two, Venkatraman one, Kolmar none and holds his for the closing section. None of the Nature papers gives the reader any advice.

## One conflict, resolved

Your writing rules ban sentences over 30 words. That governs replies to you, not the manuscript. Every one of the nineteen papers runs a median Results sentence of 16 to 28 words with a quarter of sentences over 30, and enforcing 30 in the paper would make it read unlike anything in this literature. Rule 16 is the manuscript version: median 20 to 26, ceiling about 55, and the long ones are lists of names.

## The specification

Twenty rules. Each one has a check you can run on a draft. Sources are Kolmar & Grulke 2021, Dablander et al. 2023 and FP-ADMET 2021 in Journal of Cheminformatics; Heid 2023, Hirschfeld 2020, Scalia 2020 and Landrum & Riniker 2024 in JCIM; Friesacher 2025 and Svensson 2025; and nine Nature Machine Intelligence papers used mostly as counter-examples.

### Naming and numbers

**1. No name for a derived quantity.** Print the two raw values, or say in words what was computed. Check: grep for a noun standing in for a difference, ratio or loss that is not R2, RMSE, Spearman, AUC_norm, coverage or one of the seven uncertainty statistics — swing, cost, penalty, damage, budget, spread — plus the existing bans, arm, stage, retention area, descriptor vector, read-across.
*Kolmar & Grulke run noise at 15 levels on 8 datasets and coin one quantity in the whole paper. Dablander coins none: QSAR-MAE, AC-MCC, PD-accuracy are a standard metric plus the task. FP-ADMET reports 1500 models over 75 endpoints and 20 fingerprints and coins none.*

**2. AUC_norm gets four sentences before its first number.** What it is the area under, what the bottom axis is, which direction is better, and what a high and a low value each mean. Clean R2 and R2 at the top noise level sit beside it.
*Kolmar reads his ratio at both ends before any table, and prints the raw pair with the derived one: "3.3 for SVR and 6.1 for RF... different by a factor of 1.8". Scalia states the blind spot of an area metric where the metric is defined.*

**3. Never "X is robust".** Name what it did, on which dataset, under which noise type, at which level. Check: search for is/are/was/were followed by robust, superior, best, stable, reliable, sensitive, tolerant.
*Kolmar's single robustness sentence carries two scope clauses. Dablander splits by task: "while GINs appear to be inferior to ECFPs for QSAR-prediction, they tend to be advantageous for AC-classification".*

**4. Every count says what the top and the bottom count, in words, before the number.** "13 out of the 21 settings" (Friesacher), "5 out of 11... MoleculeNet benchmark datasets" (Boulougouri), "all mk = 3 * 2 = 6 hyperparameter-optimised models" (Dablander).

**5. Anchor the noise levels once to a published assay error in log units**, in the Results, so a reader can see which level is one unit of real error. The fraction-of-training-spread scale is stated alongside, never instead.
*Kolmar: "1.1 log units of noise were added, or 1.6 times the average standard deviation reported in ChemBL". The project's sourced figures: pIC50 0.68, pKi and hERG 0.54, Caco-2 0.35, logD 0.15 within-lab.*

### Claims

**6. Grade the verb to the evidence.** Measured takes show, found, observed. A reading takes indicates, suggests. An untested mechanism takes could, might, may — once, and never restated later as established.

*Ektefaie: "The purpose of this study is not to rank models by their AUSPCs... To rank models requires averaging over many AUSPCs."*

**8. A null is stated flatly, kept, and allowed to change the recommendation.** A baseline matching a more elaborate method goes in the abstract.
*Hirschfeld's headline is a null. Friesacher's baseline MLP matching the uncertainty methods drives his final recommendation.*

**9. Unexplained stays unexplained**, in one sentence, naming the exact cases. Kolmar: "It remains unclear why this behavior is different for the Solv and Tox134 datasets."

**10. Against expectation: expectation first, result second**, two sentences at most, one adverb of surprise inside the finding sentence. Landrum names the expectation, then says the results did not meet it, then offers a cause marked "it seems... perhaps".

**11. At most three recommendations in the paper.** One sentence each, conditional, last sentence of the block that earned it, with the cost attached. No recommendations section.

**12. Engage prior work where the result appears**, naming the study and saying agrees, extends or contradicts. Kolmar: "the current work complements and extends those initial studies".

# What is reported, and how

## First, the question you asked

"Shared offset condition cost" is defined in `PAPER_REVISION_GUIDE_FINAL.md` at line 6493 and the line under it. It is the R² lost across the noise range under the grouped-shifted condition, over and above the R² lost under plain Gaussian. It is a median over the models that ran, one value per dataset per representation. Your reading was nearly right. It is a difference between two conditions, not a degradation across conditions. The number is fine. The name is not, and no paper in the set names a quantity that way.

One to decode rather than remove. "Held representation" heads six table columns. It is a condition of the analysis, not a metric, so it needs one sentence at first use saying the results below are the ones computed at that representation.

## The test that catches the next one

Any quantity name must decompose into a standard statistic plus the data it was computed on, or be written as a formula. AUC_norm, R²(clean), R²(σ=1.5) and ΔR² all pass. A name built from a condition plus an English abstraction fails. That is rule 2 below, and it is the one rule that every one of the nineteen papers obeys: Kolmar has RMSE_noise and m_noise/m_true, Dablander has QSAR-MAE and AC-MCC, Svensson has ΔNLL, Scalia spells AUCO out as Area Under the Confidence-Oracle error.

If you want it enforced rather than remembered, `scripts/check_bib_and_docs.py` already fails on a rule of this kind. Adding a word list to it is one function, no re-runs and nothing from the cluster. Say the word and it goes in.

## The twenty-five rules

They are in the structured list, each with the papers and the quotes behind it. Grouped, they are: four on naming, seven on numbers in text, three on tables and figures, six on structure and takeaways, two on anchoring the noise scale, one on keeping the two halves of an uncertainty estimate apart, one on appraisal words, and one on citing prior work inside the paragraph.

Three of them answer your standing complaint directly. Rule 5 caps prose values at about two per 1,000 words of Results and discussion. Rule 17 says each subsection ends with one plain sentence a reader can act on, which is Dablander's habit and Heid's, not an invention. Rule 18 says the number and what it means go in separate sentences.

## Where the two journals disagree

Three places, and the target journal wins each one.

Number density. Dablander runs 1.7 measured values per 1,000 words of results prose, Kolmar 2.4, Hirschfeld zero. Mataraso runs 21.9 and Ektefaie 13.6. Follow the first set.

Exact cells against bands. Venkatraman's whole results carry three performance values and all three are band edges. Dablander's six are hedged levels. The Nature Machine Intelligence papers print exact cells. Follow the first set.

Coined metric names. Ektefaie coins six terms and defines each one. The six Journal of Cheminformatics papers in this set coin no measurement at all between them, and the closest analogue to your study keeps its headline quantity as a formula. Follow the first set. AUC_norm is your one exception and it earns it, because it is named after the operation and the curve.

One correction to the brief I was handed: the batches cover six Journal of Cheminformatics papers, not three — Kolmar 2021, Venkatraman 2021, Mervin 2021, Lapins 2018, Dablander 2023 and Wang 2023. That strengthens the three findings above rather than changing them.

## Two things only you can decide

Whether AUC_norm carries the paper, or R² at named noise levels does. Kolmar puts raw RMSE curves in figures for two datasets and his fitted slopes in tables for all eight. Your equivalent is raw R² curves in figures and AUC_norm per cell in tables. The alternative is to lead on R² at two or three named noise levels and keep AUC_norm as one summary column. The first costs nothing, since both already exist. The second means new tables from the figure script and an ARC re-run.

Whether the near-zero-number style is what you want at this size. Your grid is larger than any of the six target-journal papers. Dablander's style puts every value in figures and gives the text the ordering only. Mervin's, also Journal of Cheminformatics, runs about fourteen values per 1,000 words because his independent variable is itself a standard deviation, which is closer to your case. Both are publishable in the same journal. The rules above hold under either.
